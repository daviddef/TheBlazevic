# The index calls them Piberich, and that is why nobody found them

**20 September 2026.** Ahnentafel 52, *Josephus Pilipić*, was baptised at Karlobag on
**11 March 1768**. The archive has held that date from the tree since the beginning and
never had the entry. Three sittings looked for it. It was in the index the whole time,
filed under a surname that exists nowhere else in the parish.

    11 March 1768 · «Josephum PIBERICH» · father «Jacobi Piberich» · mother «Catharina»
    Karlobag, Lika-Senj · ark 1:1:QKMX-YX3G
    Croatia, Church Books, 1516-1994 / Croatia, Lika-Senj, Births, from 1689 to 1898

## Why this is the right man and not a coincidence

**«Piberich» is a hapax.** A surname search for *Piberich* across Karlobag returns
**one record** — this one. It is not a family in this parish. It is one clerk's reading
of one hand.

**The same couple appear three more times, under two other spellings.** Querying
*father Jacob × mother Catharina* at Karlobag, with **no surname at all**, returns four
children and no others:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 21 October 1758 | Simonem **Pillopich** | `QKMX-YDW1` |
| 28 July 1762 | Franciscum **Pillepich** | `QKMX-BSB4` |
| 1 March 1765 | Simolicianum **Pillepich** | `QKMX-BMMQ` |
| **11 March 1768** | Josephum **Piberich** — **ahnentafel 52** | `QKMX-YX3G` |

**And «Pillepich» is this family's own attested spelling**, from a page this archive read
itself. Ahnentafel 104's death entry of 10 April 1778 reads
«**Pillepich** Jacobus *Capitaneus portûs*», and ahnentafel 105's of 29 May 1790 reads
«Catharina annorum 70, **vidua defti Jacobi [P]ilepich**». The couple in the death
register and the couple in the baptism index are written the same way, and the 1768 date
matches the tree to the day.

## What it does not give

**The index carries no surname for the mother** — she is «Catharina» and nothing more.
Catta Luckinić's surname still comes from the tree, not from these entries. And **no
image has been opened**, so the reading is filed `INDEX ONLY`: this same indexing has
already been caught naming the wrong mother for ahnentafel 106 and the wrong father for
Marijan Blažević. The house number and the *Conditio* column are still on the film.

## The method, which is the part worth keeping

Every earlier sitting searched **by surname**, and the surname is the one thing a bad
hand destroys. Row 54 already knew the index writes this family *Pidepics* and
*Pilleppics*; it tried six spellings and found a 1739 entry and nothing else. Six was
never going to be enough, because **no list of guesses contains «Piberich»**.

**Drop the surname.** `q.givenName` + `q.fatherGivenName` + `q.motherGivenName` +
a date window + the parish finds a household whatever the clerk did to the family name.
It is how these four came out in one query after three sittings of guessing.

## A correction to row 26

Row 26 has said since 15 September that ahnentafel 105 is the last unread Karlobag
ancestor and that reaching her «is a walk, not a lookup», the 1691–1770 book being
«not meaningfully indexed for this surname». **Both halves need amending.** She has held
a direct reading — her own death of 1790 — for some time, so she was not unread. And the
book *is* indexed for this family; it is indexed under a name nobody would search for.

## One negative from the same sitting, stated as a negative

**Ahnentafel 110, Michael Gerkacs, born about 1762, is still not found.** The same
surname-free query — *Michael, father Nicolaus, Karlobag, 1757–1767* — returns nothing
of his, and ten spellings of Gerkacs return no Karlobag baptism between 1721 and 1773.
**Ahnentafel 221 remains absent from the tree entirely**: Michael has a father and no
mother, and his baptism is the entry that would name her. It was not found today.

**And a hypothesis that was tested and failed.** Both surnames showing the same gap —
Pilipić silent 1739–1810, Gerkacs silent 1721–1773 — looked like a hole in the index
covering exactly the generation of row 54's four walls. **It is not.** A decade census of
Karlobag, 1690 to 1780, returns 100 Karlobag baptisms out of every 100 fetched in every
decade. The index is dense there. The gaps were spelling, not coverage — which is what
sent this sitting at the surname-free query in the first place.
