WHY THIS IS A HOLDINGS QUESTION AND NOT A RESEARCH REQUEST
===========================================================================

One question is now asked from two directions and answered from neither.

  * The PARISH says the registers went to the Archdiocese, and that the
    Archdiocese passed them to the State Archive.
  * The STATE ARCHIVE replied the same day that it does not undertake research
    of this complexity. It did not say what it holds — which is fair, because
    I had asked it to search rather than to list.

So nobody has yet said whether these books SURVIVE, or where they now are. The
Archdiocese is the one party that handed them over and would hold the transfer
record. That record is what I am asking for.

I have read the filmed copies (Hrvatski državni arhiv, through FamilySearch)
leaf by leaf. Every item below is something the films do NOT cover, and I say in
each case exactly where the film stops. I am not asking anyone to repeat work I
can do myself.

===========================================================================
1. SV. JAKOV KRMPOTE — sixty-four catalogued years that are not on the film
===========================================================================

The FamilySearch waypoint for Krmpote-Vodice is catalogued "Births 1815-1896".
THE FILM ITSELF BEGINS IN 1879: image 10 is page 2 and reads «Godine 1879». I
checked it leaf by leaf.

  1a. Does a BAPTISMAL REGISTER for Sv. Jakov Krmpote covering the years
      BEFORE 1879 survive, and who holds it now?

  1b. Does any MARRIAGE REGISTER for that parish survive, of any period?
      No marriage register for Sv. Jakov Krmpote is filmed at all — not a gap
      in a range, the event type is simply absent.

What hangs on it: JURAJ «JURKO» BLAŽEVIĆ, a seljak of Smokvica Krmpotska house
114, is named in your register in 1895 — «Jurko Blažević i Tereza rođ.
Žubrinić, selj.», at the baptism of their son Marijan, 12 April 1895, page 168,
entry 26. His baptism, most likely 1855-1870, would name his father outright.
Five further generations, back to a Mateša Blažević born about 1750, rest on a
link no document yet states.

===========================================================================
2. RIJEKA, 1892 — a gap in the film, twice over
===========================================================================

LJUBOMIR «LJUBO» BLAŽEVIĆ was born at RIJEKA on 1 MARCH 1892. The filmed Rijeka
parish registers (koha:722509) run 1594-1920, and their own list of volumes
gives the baptisms as:

        ... Rodjeni 1880-1886 · 1886-1890 · 1890-1891 · 1900-1901 ...

There is no 1892-1899 baptismal volume on the film, and the alphabetical Kazalo
skips the same span — 1800-1889, then 1895-1902. The entry falls into a gap by
image and by index both.

  2a. Does a RIJEKA BAPTISMAL REGISTER covering 1 MARCH 1892 survive, and in
      whose care is it now?

A Krmpote peasant household had a child in Rijeka in 1892 and then went home —
the next child is baptised back at Krmpote in 1893. The entry would give the
address, and the address would explain why.

===========================================================================
3. THE TRANSFER RECORD ITSELF
===========================================================================

  3a. If there is a PRIMOPREDAJNI ZAPISNIK or an inventory of what the
      Archdiocese handed to the State Archive — for the Crikvenica deanery, or
      for Sv. Jakov Krmpote specifically — a copy of that one document would
      answer 1a, 1b and 2a together, and would tell me which volumes to ask
      the State Archive for BY SHELFMARK rather than by description.

That last point is why I am writing to you rather than back to Rijeka. An
archive that will not search on a description will often produce a volume that
is named. I do not yet know the names.
