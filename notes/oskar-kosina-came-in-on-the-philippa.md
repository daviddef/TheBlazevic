# Oskar Kosina came in on the *Philippa*, and he was already married

**21 September 2026.** The Argentine trail was declared empty this morning — *Studia Croatica*,
Find a Grave, the community's own literature, nothing. **It was empty because the right database
had not been searched.** CEMLA, the Buenos Aires port arrival books, is free, covers **1882–1960**
and holds **4.4 million people**. It is behind a CAPTCHA, so a person had to do it.

**Twelve rows come back for KOSINA. One of them is ours.**

> **KOSINA · OSCAR · 25 · C · YUGOSLAVA/S.H.S. · SEGKA · COLONO · 1947/10/09 · PHILIPPA · GENOVA**

## What that single line gives

| | |
|---|---|
| **He arrived 9 October 1947** | The archive had no arrival at all. He died **6 years and 3 months later**. |
| **Aged 25** | Born about 1922 — agrees with the tree and with his memorial card. |
| **«SEGKA»** | **SEGNA — the Italian name for Senj.** A Buenos Aires clerk wrote down an Italian form of a Croatian town, which is what you get when a man boards at Genoa. |
| **«COLONO»** | *Settler*. The visa class for post-war agricultural immigration, which is how displaced Europeans entered Argentina in 1947. |
| **From GENOA** | The route out of the Italian displaced-persons camps. Not a voyage from home; **an exit**. |
| **«C» — CASADO** | **HE WAS ALREADY MARRIED THE DAY HE LANDED.** |

## The last of those is the one that changes the work

This archive has been looking for Oskar's wife in **Buenos Aires**, on the assumption that a
twenty-five-year-old arriving alone married there. **He did not.** He was married before he
sailed — **in Europe, in the camps or in Croatia** — and she either travelled with him, followed
him, or is on the same manifest under her maiden name.

**So the next document is not an Argentine marriage register. It is the passenger list of the
*Philippa*, 9 October 1947.** Everyone who sailed with him is on those pages.

## And he was not the only Kosina on that ship

> **KOSINA · FRAJNO · 47 · C · CROATA (HRVATSKA) · AGRONOMO · 1947/04/01 · PHILIPPA · GENOVA**

**FRAJNO is FRANJO.** Born about **1900**, married, an **agronomist** — and he crossed on **the
same ship, out of the same port, six months earlier**.

**Two men of one surname, the same vessel, the same year, the same exit route.** That is not
coincidence; it is either a family or a network, and probably both.

**Is he a brother?** Martin Kozina and Angjelika Boras had sons in **1891** and **1894**, and she
died in **1902** — so **a son born about 1900 is possible**. It is possible and it is not
established, and he is recorded in `sources/found.psv` as exactly that.

**One detail of his is worth keeping.** His nationality is entered **«CROATA (HRVATSKA)»** where
every other Yugoslav on these pages is «YUGOSLAVA/S.H.S.». **In April 1947 that is a man telling
a clerk what he was, and the clerk writing it down.**

## The other nine rows, and one that had to be checked

* **A whole household, 10 April 1911**, off the *Sofia Hohenberg* from **Trieste**, all entered
  **«AUSTRIACO - DALMATA»**: **GERKO 30** and **CLARA 25**, with **ANKA 4, IRMA 4, OJESKOSLAIO 2**
  and **GELKA 0**. *Gerko* is **Jerko**, *Ojeskoslaio* is **Vjekoslav**, *Gelka* is **Jelka**; the
  two four-year-olds are twins or one age is wrong. **Croatian names, Adriatic Austrians, sailing
  from Trieste — and nothing but the surname joins them to this family.**
* **IVAN KOSINA, 27, single, 30 November 1928**, from **Hamburg**, born «VELIC», a farm labourer.
  **He is NOT Ivan Christian.** Oskar's son was born in Argentina after October 1947; this man was
  born about **1901**. The names collide and the dates cannot.
* **MICHAL KOSINA, 42, Polish, 15 January 1929**, from Cherbourg — the Polish Kosina, of the
  village in the Subcarpathian, and no relation.
* **IVAN and MICHAL each appear twice.** CEMLA indexes some arrivals from two books.

**No wife and no son under this surname.** She would be under her maiden name; he was not born yet.
