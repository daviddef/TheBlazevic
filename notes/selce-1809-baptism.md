# Selce, 22 March 1809 — ahnentafel 25, and a ninth by-name

Read 14 September 2026 from `photos/line/4505562-mariam-jellichic-baptism-1809.jpg`,
**3,022 × 2,230**, on disk since the media sweep and marked *scanned* — an image
attached, never read out.

Latin prose baptismal register, *Anno 1809*, both pages numbered and entries
numbered in the margin. Entry **70**, house **110**:

> *Die 22. Martii Aº 1809. baptizavi ego **Michaël Rombol** Parochus Loci
> infantem lege natam patre **Georgio**, Matre **Margarita Jellichich <u>dictis
> Jurešina</u>**, nomine **Mariam**. Susceptoribus adolescente **Leopoldo
> Jellichich**, et **Agnete consorte Vincentii Lonzarich <u>Hel</u> dicti**.*

**Ahnentafel 25, baptised 22 March 1809** — the tree's date exactly — daughter
of **ahnentafel 50 and 51**, named by their first names, at **house 110**.

## The ninth by-name, and it is underlined

***Jellichich dictis Jurešina*** — Jelličić, **called Jurešina** — and the
scribe has **underlined it**, exactly as his successor underlined *Antich Papa*
in 1835 and 1838. The godmother's husband is *Lonzarich **Hel** dicti*,
underlined too.

That is now **nine by-name pairs from this one parish**, in three different
hands across thirty-three years:

    Antich       aliter / dictus  Papa
    Antich       aliter           Dudich        (twice, 1835 and 1842)
    Jellichich   dictis           Jurešina
    Lonzarich    dicti            Hel
    Franich      aliter           Lonzarich
    Puxich       aliter           Lonzarich
    Badrovich    aliter           Bagatello
    Penes        aliter           Paschul

**Question 5 is no longer an argument about one family.** Selce recorded a
household by-name beside the surname as a matter of course, underlined it to
mark what it was, and did so for at least six different surnames. *Papa* is one
instance of a parish-wide system.

## And a possible discriminator for question 13b

The Jelličići of house 110 are **dicti Jurešina**. The Margareta Jelličić *uxor
Georgii* buried on 8 May 1842 is at **house 204** and carries **no by-name** in
her entry.

That is suggestive and **not conclusive** — a death entry is terser than a
baptism, and by-names are dropped as often as they are used. But it is the first
thing found that could separate two Jelličić households rather than merely
failing to join them.

Houses, for the record, all in one line of descent:

    1809  baptism of the daughter   house 110
    1832  death of Georgius         house  39
    1842  death of Margareta        house 204

Three houses in thirty-three years. Either the family moved twice, or Selce
renumbered, or these are not all the same household. **The house number is
therefore not a reliable identifier in this parish**, which weakens the argument
made from it yesterday in both directions.

## Also

The priest is **Michaël Rombol** in 1809 and **Vincentius Rombal** in 1835 and
1838 — a family of parish priests at Selce across at least a generation.
