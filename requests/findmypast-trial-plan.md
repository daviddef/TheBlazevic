# FindMyPast — what to do with two days

**Written 13 September 2026.** The trial expires in two days, so this is ordered
by value, not by tidiness.

## The one thing to know first

**FindMyPast holds no Croatian records.** It is a UK / Ireland / US / Australia
service. It cannot touch the Otočac, Senj or Krmpote problem — not the
[92 islands](../site/src/pages/buckets.astro), not the Žubrinić fragments, not the
house numbers. Anyone hoping this subscription will join the family tree together
should stop hoping that now.

**What it does hold is the emigration**, and that is worth real money to us.

## Why the Australian records matter more than they look

Nineteen of the twenty-one Australians in this archive sit in **one island of 41
people, 1765–1924, that is entirely disconnected from Hedviga.** The two oldest
people in that island are **Casparus Žubrinić, born 14 January 1767** and
**Helena Žubrinić, born 4 November 1765** — and Casparus is the man whose father,
in our tree, is an entry literally called *“Brothers of Casparus (It Seems)”*.

So the Australian branch is not a side-story. It is **one of the two halves that
will not join**, and everything it tells us about where these people came from
narrows the Croatian search we cannot do here.

**Australian death and marriage registrations name the parents, including the
mother's maiden name, and often the birthplace.** That is the lever.

## Priority 1 — Ivan / John Žubrinić, the emigrant himself

**Born 6 March 1860. Died 15 December 1930 at Port Pirie, South Australia.**
Our record says he was born at **Senj**; the whole family is **Otočac**. That
contradiction is unresolved and it matters.

| # | Search | What we want off it |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **SA death registration 1930**, John/Ivan Zubrinich, Port Pirie | **Parents' names**, age, birthplace, years in the colony |
| 1.2 | **SA marriage c.1884–85**, Zubrinich | **Both sets of parents**, his stated birthplace, occupation |
| 1.3 | **Naturalisation papers** | Usually the **exact birth village** — the single most valuable line in the whole trial |
| 1.4 | **Passenger / shipping lists c.1880–85** | Ship, port, date, who he travelled with |

A probe search for `zubrinich · john · died 1930 ±2` already returns **32 hits**,
so the records are there.

## Priority 2 — the other eighteen Australians

All Zubrinich, Port Pirie / Port Germein / Quorn / Whyalla / Broken Hill /
Ardrossan / Solomontown, born 1885–1924. For each: **birth, marriage, death**.

Two specific jobs while in there:

- **Katalyn Zubrinich and Kate Zubrinich** are in our tree as two people with the
  *same* birth (26 July 1886) and the *same* death (23 January 1927, Quorn). One
  registration would prove they are one woman.
- **Robert Emmett Zubrinich**, born and died 1888 at Port Germein, and
  **Susannah**, born April and died December 1889 — infant deaths that would
  confirm the family was at Port Germein between the Port Pirie children.

## Priority 3 — occupations, which we are short of

Our tree records a trade for **16 people out of 1,245**. **Australian electoral
rolls** list occupation and address by year, and they run from 1903. Nineteen
people × several decades is potentially more occupational data than the entire
rest of the archive holds. It feeds [/work](../site/src/pages/work.astro)
directly.

## Priority 4 — the Boras branch, which nobody has looked at

- **Mathew John Boras**, b. 1892 **Yarraville, Victoria**, d. 2 May 1923
  **Marrickville, NSW**
- **David John Boras**, b. 1919 **Sydney**, d. **Kurri Kurri, NSW**

Boras is one of only two families that connects to Hedviga without scaffolding,
and this Australian limb has never been researched at all.

## Priority 5 — newspapers, if time remains

FindMyPast carries British and some Australian papers. **Port Pirie** was a
smelter town with a local press. Worth trying:

- `Zubrinich` in Australian papers 1884–1955 — obituaries name relatives
- the **1927 death of Katalyn/Kate at Quorn**
- any notice of Ivan's **1930** death

## What NOT to spend the trial on

- Croatian anything. It is not there.
- Hedviga and Ivan Defranceski's **1948 emigration to South Africa** — FindMyPast's
  South African coverage is thin, and this is better chased through the
  destination country.
- Confirming dates we already hold. The archive has enough of those; what it
  lacks is **joins** and **occupations**.

## One practical obstacle

The Chrome the assistant can drive is **not logged in** to FindMyPast — it shows
*Log in / Sign up*. The session must be in another browser or another Chrome
profile. **Logging in within that same Chrome window** would let the searches
above be run directly; otherwise they have to be run by hand.
