# Državni arhiv u Gospiću — Otočac, the Žubrinići of Šumećica

**To:** Državni arhiv u Gospiću, Kaniška 17, 53000 Gospić
**zahtjevi@arhiv-gospic.hr**
*(Otočac is in Lika-Senj and is not one of the towns excepted from Gospić's
remit — see `WHERE-TO-WRITE.md`. The Senj material belongs to Rijeka; this does
not.)*

**Status: drafted, not sent.**

## What is wanted, in one line

Two entries from the **Otočac** registers that would show whether **one man or
two** stands behind the name *Michaël / Mate Žubrinić* between 1834 and 1880.

## The problem, stated plainly

Our family record gives one couple — **Michaël Žubrinić and Maria née
Orešković** — two sons:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Nicolaus**, baptised **24 January 1834** | *filius legitimus*, *Confiniarius ex Pago Shumechicza N° 24*, son of **Michaël Xubrinich et Maria uxor ejus nata Oreskovich** |
| **Nikola**, born **23 August 1880** | named at his own marriage (Senj, 3 July 1910) as son of **††Mate Žubrinić i Marija rodj. Orešković, poljodjelci** |

**Forty-six years separate them, and both entries name parents of the same two
names.** A third entry, the baptism of **Josephus** on **16 March 1845**, gives
the mother as **Maria Žubrinić *nata Marković***.

So either one man married two women both called Maria, or — more likely — there
were **two men of the same name a generation apart**, and our record has merged
them.

## The two documents that would settle it

1. **Marriage of a Michaël / Mate Žubrinić to a Maria Orešković, Otočac,
   before 1834.** This is the elder couple.
2. **A second such marriage, Otočac, roughly 1855–1870.** If one exists, the two
   couples are proved distinct and the question is closed. If the registers hold
   only one, the merge is real but differently shaped.

## Also wanted, if those volumes are open

- **Death of a Maria Žubrinić *née Orešković*, Otočac, between 1834 and 1845** —
  which would explain the second wife *née Marković*.
- **Marriage of Michaël Žubrinić and Maria Marković**, Otočac, **1834–1845**.
- The **baptism of Nikola Žubrinić, 23 August 1880, Otočac** — his own entry,
  which names his father directly and would date that father.

## What is already known, so the search can be narrow

| | |
|---|---|
| **Village** | **Šumećica**, house **N° 24**, in 1834 — a *Confiniarius* household of the Military Frontier |
| **Later** | the 1880 family are *poljodjelci* at **Otočac** itself, and both parents are dead by July 1910 |
| **The 1880 son** | **Nikola Žubrinić**, *kontrolor gradske daće* — municipal excise controller — married **Olga Štimac** at Senj, 3 July 1910 |
| **Other Žubrinić offices at Otočac** | *Vigiliarum Magister pensionatus* (1834), *Sylvarum Custos in Shumechicza* (1834), *Sylvarum Custos* of the Otočac company (1835), *Custos Carcerum* (1845), and a **Forest Ranger** born 1837 |
| **Related surnames in the same entries** | Orešković, Marković, Kostelac, Untersinger, Ratković, Kranjčević |

## Why it matters, briefly

**Maria Catharina Orešković**, born at **Dubrava N° 29 on 8 September 1835**, is
carried in our record as Nicolaus's mother. She cannot be: he was baptised
nineteen months before she was born. Her own baptism names the parents our
record gives her, so she is a real person attached to the wrong generation — and
if the two couples above are distinct, **she belongs to the younger one**, and
her whole ancestry with her.

One marriage entry, or the absence of a second, decides four generations.

## And one request that is not about a person

The **Otočac death register for any decade between 1830 and 1900** — any decade
at all, we are not asking for a name.

We have read the Senj death register and not a single page of any village book,
and one of our own published findings depends on that gap. Our analysis shows the
Senj families with their child deaths recorded and the village families with
theirs invisible — and we cannot tell whether that means the villages lost fewer
children or simply that nobody has read their books. **A sample of any village
death register would settle it**, and if the answer is that the villages recorded
their deaths just as fully, we will withdraw the finding.


## And a class of book we now know survives: *status animarum*

A **status animarum** — the parish's house-by-house census of souls — would
answer more of the above than any single entry, because it lists a household
together rather than one baptism at a time.

We are not asking blind. A page of one survives in our own family papers,
captioned **Our Lady of the Snow, Krivi Put** and running from 1897 to 1939,
with each person entered as *ž.* (wife), *s.* (son), *k.* (daughter) or *i.*
(child of) beneath the head of the household, and a right-hand column carrying
cross-references of the form *Iz … 43* and *v. br. 18/51*. So we know the books
were kept in this region and that at least one has survived into private hands.

**If any status animarum volume survives for the parishes below, a scan of the
relevant household pages would be worth more to us than several individual
entries:**

- **Otočac**, and the filial village of **Šumećica** — especially any volume
  covering **1830–1885**. A household page would show at a stroke whether one
  *Michaël / Mate Žubrinić* or two stands behind these entries, since a census
  lists the man, his wife and his living children on one line of sight. This is
  the single most useful thing that could reach us.

*A second, unrelated request, if it is no trouble:* the **Otočac death register
for 1845**, for **Elias “Ilija” Žubrinić** and **Ana Untersinger**, who both
died that year leaving seven children. Two adults dying in one year suggests an
epidemic; the cause, if it is given, would be worth knowing. *Untersinger* is a
German surname in Lika and we have not traced it.
