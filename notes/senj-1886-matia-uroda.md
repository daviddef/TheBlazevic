**This is ahnentafel 27**, and three things in it are new.

**Her husband gets an occupation.** Ahnentafel 26 is written here as *«Vicko
Pilipić, **fin. stražar u miru**»* — a **retired finance guard**, a customs or
excise man of the Military Frontier's civil successor. Nothing in this archive
had said what he did.

**Her origin is recorded as Karlobag**, which independently confirms the
birthplace the tree carries. She is the daughter born **23 April 1810** whose
birth date fixed her parents' marriage to before 1810 — and that marriage was
found on 15 September as **17 June 1807** (`notes/karlobag-1807-uroda-gergas.md`).
Karlobag to Senj is the move her life makes, and the death register is the first
document to state it.

**And she is indexed under the wrong surname**, which is why she was missed. The
FamilySearch index files her as **Pilipić**, her *married* name; a search for
*Uroda* returns nothing at Senj. **A married woman's death is indexed under her
husband's surname**, and any sweep for a maiden name will walk straight past her.

## One thing recorded as read rather than as fact

The register gives her age as **78**. Born 23 April 1810 and dead 9 February
1886, she was **75**. A two- or three-year overstatement is ordinary in these
columns — the age is what the informant said, not what a document said — so it
is recorded as written and not treated as a correction to the birth date, which
has a baptism behind it and the age has not.
