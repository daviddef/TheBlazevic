# Drawing Tereza Žubrinić's mother's family — what there is, and how to get at it

14 September 2026. **Ahnentafel 11, Ana "Shambul" Marić, is the emptiest person
on the direct line.** The archive holds a name and two tree links. No birth, no
death, no place, no parish. Her father (ahnentafel 22) is a **surname with no
given name**, and her mother (ahnentafel 23) **is not in the tree at all.**

This is the plan for getting past her, written while FamilySearch is blocked so
that the next sitting is a tick-list rather than a search.

---

## 1. The name is probably not her name

MyHeritage carries her as **“Ana Shambul Marić”** and, on the other tree,
**“Ana Žubrinić rođen Shumbul Marić”**. Two spellings of the same odd first
element.

**This archive has seen that shape before.** Across ten surnames and
eighty years it has documented the *nadimak* — the household name written beside
the surname and joined by *aliter*, *dictus* or *dicti*, and **underlined by the
priest**: *Antich <u>Papa</u>*, *Jellichich dictis <u>Jurešina</u>*,
*Antich <u>Dudin</u>*, *Loncsarich <u>Franich</u>*.

**“Shumbul Marić” is that pattern flattened into a double surname by a tree
program.** If it is, then:

* the surname to search is **Marić**, and *Šumbul* is the **house name**;
* the house name is the only thing that separates this family from every other
  Marić in Lika — and Marić is one of the commonest surnames in the region;
* and the by-name **will be written in the register**, which makes the entries
  below worth reading closely rather than skimming for a surname.

**It is a hypothesis and is not being added to the by-names data**, which holds
only phrases actually read off a page.

## 2. What is reachable, in order of cost

The couple's children in the tree are **Lucca (1853)**, **Marija (about 15
September 1858)**, **Jacobus (undated)** and **Tereza (29 November 1867)**.
Otočac births are filmed **1834–1858**. So:

**a. Lucca's baptism, 1853, and Marija's, 1858 — both inside the filmed book.**
This is the cheapest step and the best. The Otočac entries this archive has
already read are laid out:

> *Confiniarius, ex pago Shumechicza N° 24* — **Michaël Xubrinich** et **Maria
> uxor ejus nata Oreskovich**

— that is, **the mother is named with her maiden surname**, and the household is
fixed by **village and house number**. Two entries would give Ana her surname in
a register rather than in a tree, and would place her household.
**Marija's falls in 1858, the last year the film covers.**

**b. Ana's own baptism, Otočac, about 1834–1838.** If she bore a child in 1853
she was born by about 1835 — which is **inside** the filmed range, but only just.
Her baptism names **both her parents**, which is two walls in one entry.

**c. The Otočac *Stanje duša* 1710–1846.** The census sitting already has its
method — the contrast filter on the *Pagus* margin, and *Domus* numbers to work
from. A **Marić household** at Otočac or Šumećica would sit in it with dates of
birth for everybody in the house.

## 3. What is not reachable, and should be said so

* **The marriage of Nikola and Ana.** If Lucca is theirs they married by 1852,
  and the Otočac marriage film begins in **1859**. The book exists in Croatian
  custody; it is not in this collection.
* **Tereza's own baptism of 1867.** Otočac births stop at **1858**. The one
  document that would name her mother in her own right is nine years outside.

## 4. Two things to check before building on any of it

**Nikola was nineteen when Lucca was born.** He was baptised 24 January 1834;
Lucca is dated 1853. That is possible and it is also the shape of a wrong
attachment — the same confusion as the two men called Michaël in question 4d.
**Lucca and Marija may belong to a different Žubrinić couple**, and the 1853 and
1858 baptisms would settle that too.

**Lucca's birthplace in the tree is a conflated string** —
*“Otocac/Smokvica Krmpotska, Općina Novi…”* — two places a hundred kilometres
apart joined into one field. It should not be read as evidence of either.

## 5. What was tried while blocked, and returned nothing

**Arcanum** — the Hungarian-administration press, which gave this archive a great
deal a few hours earlier — holds nothing here. *«Šumbul» Otočac*: **0**.
*«Šambul»*: 348 hits, every one a Moroccan legionnaire tried at Cluj in 1936.
*«Marić» Otočac*: 149 hits, all twentieth-century passport notices and none of
them ours. **Marić is too common a surname for a press search without a given
name and a date** — which is exactly why the *Šumbul* by-name matters.

# The Otočac baptisms, done — and the by-name has evidence

14 September 2026, the same day. FamilySearch came back and the plan above was
carried out. It did not go the way the plan expected, and it went better.

## The thing the plan missed: these baptisms are INDEXED

**Otočac baptisms 1846–1858 are in FamilySearch's record index, with both
parents named and the mother's maiden surname given.** Ninety-three Žubrinić
baptisms at Otočac between 1846 and 1860. This archive has been reading Otočac
off film for a fortnight and **nobody had searched the index.**

## Two children of a Nicolaus Žubrinić, and only two

Filtering the index to a father called **Nicolaus**:

> **Maria Zubrinić** — baptised **15 September 1858**, Otočac —
> parents **Nicolaus Zubrinić** and **Anna Marić**

> **Stephanus «Bubrinić»** — baptised **3 July 1856**, Otočac — line 49 —
> parents **Nicolaus «Bubrinić»** and **Anna «Strumbulko»**

*Bubrinić* is the index's misreading of **Žubrinić**; the initial is the same
shape in this hand.

**The first confirms the tree to the day.** The tree carries Marija Žubrinić as
*ABT 15 SEP 1858* — the "about" can come off — and it gives ahnentafel 11 her
surname, **Marić**, from a register rather than from a family tree.

**The second is a child this archive did not have at all**, and it carries the
other half of her name.

## «Strumbulko» is unique in the whole collection

Searched across **Croatia, Church Books 1516–1994** — every parish, every year —
the spelling **Strumbulko occurs exactly once**: in this entry. Every other
result the fuzzy match returns is a different name in a different county
(Struin, Struelin, Streibl, Strubl).

So the same man, in the same parish, two years apart, has a wife written
**«Anna Strumbulko»** in 1856 and **«Anna Marić»** in 1858.

**That is what a nadimak looks like from the outside.** This archive has
documented the pattern across ten surnames — *Antich <u>Papa</u>*, *Jellichich
dictis <u>Jurešina</u>*, *Antich <u>Dudin</u>* — a household name written beside
or instead of the surname, and one clerk using it where another does not.
**«Ana Shambul Marić» in the family tree is Marić plus a house name**, and the
house name is spelled **Šumbulko / Strumbulko** by the priest who wrote it.

## What was still not proved — and what the page said

**Written earlier the same day:** that the 1856 couple and the 1858 couple are
the same couple was *not* proved. Nicolaus is a common Žubrinić name at Otočac —
the same index shows Michaël, Joannes, Mathaeus, Stephanus, Franciscus,
Georgius, Jacobus and Mio Žubrinić all fathering children there in the same
decade. **The discriminator is the house number.**

**Both entries have now been read at the page. Both are the same house.**

| | 3 July 1856, entry 49 | 15 September 1858 |
|---|---|---|
| **Child** | *Stephanus, filius legitimus, Romano-Catholicus* | *Maria, filia legitima, Romano-Catholica* |
| ***Conditio*** | *Rustici Confiniarii* — **e Šumećica N° 2.** | *Rustica* — **e Šumećica N° 2.** |
| **Parents** | *Nicolaus Ž[ubrinić] et ejus uxor Anna nata **Štrumbulko**, Romano-Catholici* | *Nicolaus **Žubrinić** et ejus uxor Anna nata **Marić**, Romano-Catholici* |

In both, *Žubrinić* carries the scribe's **underline**. In both the house number
is a **single digit 2**, closed by a full stop with the rest of the cell blank —
read at full resolution against the column rule, because the neighbouring rows
on the 1858 page run *e Šumećica N° 3* and *N° 25* and a bad crop could take a
digit off either.

**One house, one man's name, one wife's given name, twenty-six months apart.
They are one couple** — and so **Anna Marić is Anna Štrumbulko**, written both
ways by the same parish. *«Ana Shambul Marić»* in the tree is **Marić plus a
house name**, and the house name is now attested on a page rather than inferred
from a tree program's punctuation.

Stephanus, 3 July 1856, is **a child this archive did not have**.

### A correction, and a new question

**There is no *Numerus Domus* column in this book.** This note claimed one
earlier today. The far-right column is ***Nrus Protocolli*** — the running entry
number, which is how the index's «line 49» resolves. **The village and the house
are written inside the *Conditio* column**, after *Rusticus* or *Confiniarius*,
in the form *e Šumećica N° 2* or *in Dubrava N° 48*. Everything above stands;
the column heading did not.

**And the house number is not the one expected.** Ahnentafel 10's own baptism,
24 January 1834, puts him in his father Michaël's *Confiniarius* household at
**Šumećica N° 24**. His children in 1856 and 1858 are at **Šumećica N° 2** —
the house the tree gives Toma Žubrinić (b. 1768).

Two readings are open and neither is being claimed:

* **He moved.** A married son leaving the *zadruga* for another house in the
  same hamlet is ordinary, and twenty-two years separate the two entries.
* **The man at house 2 is a different Nicolaus.** Against this: the 1858 entry
  matches the tree's Marija to the day, and her mother is *Marić*.

**What settles it is the *Status Animarum*, and the target has not changed** —
find Šumećica in the *Pagus* margin, then read **Domus 2 and Domus 24** together.
If one household is the other's continuation, both numbers hold the same names.

## And a negative that bears on something else

**There is no Lucca or Luca Žubrinić born 1853 to a Nicolaus in the index.**
The only Luce is *Luce Zubrinić, 30 June 1850, parents Jele Orešković and Mio
Zubrinić* — a different couple.

The tree gives Tereza a sister **Lucca born 1853**, and this archive flagged
that entry a few hours ago because it would make Nikola a father at nineteen.
**The index does not support it.**

**And the «1853 may simply be unindexed» escape has now been closed.** The
density test was run the same day: Otočac Žubrinić entries number **15 in 1851,
8 in 1852, 13 in 1853, 9 in 1854, 11 in 1855**. **1853 sits in the middle of its
neighbours** — it is covered normally. In that year there is **no Lucca and no
Luce Žubrinić baptised at Otočac, and no child at all to a father called
Nicolaus.** The only Lucia of 1853 is **Lucia *Kostelac***, daughter of
Stephanus Kostelac and Barbara Žubrinić — a Žubrinić mother, not a Žubrinić
child.

**So the entry that would make ahnentafel 10 a father at nineteen has no
register behind it.** An index is still a finding aid and not a register, and
one missed page is always possible; but the objection that made the first
negative worthless has been answered.
