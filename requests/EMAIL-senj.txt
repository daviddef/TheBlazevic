===========================================================================
1. SV. JAKOV KRMPOTE — the one link nothing states
===========================================================================

The Senj death register for 1907 (p. 225, entry 60) gives the birthplace of
TOMA BLAŽEVIĆ, nadničar, as the parish of SV. JAKOV KRMPOTE. That is the book
covering Smokvica Krmpotska, and it should hold every Blažević generation above
him.

Everything above JURAJ "JURKO" BLAŽEVIĆ — five further generations, back to a
Mateša Blažević born about 1750 — hangs on a link no document yet states. Juraj
is named as a parent by two separate Senj marriage registers (1919 and 1920) and
is placed at Smokvica house 114 in 1895. That is a strong circumstantial case. It
is not a sentence naming his father.

  1a. The BAPTISM of JURAJ / JURKO BLAŽEVIĆ, Sv. Jakov Krmpote, most likely
      1855-1870. This names his father outright.

  1b. His MARRIAGE to TEREZA ŽUBRINIĆ (b. 29 November 1867, Otočac), most
      likely 1885-1892, at Krmpote, Senj or Otočac. Names both fathers.

  1c. His DEATH, Krmpote or Senj, before December 1920 — his son's marriage
      entry of that month annotates him as dead.

What I already hold, so the search can be narrow: Juraj is a seljak, living at
SMOKVICA HOUSE 114 in 1895, entered there as "Jurko Blažević i Tereza rođ.
Žubrinić, selj." His children baptised in the parish are Ljubomir (b. Rijeka,
1 March 1892), Anka (1893), Marijan (b. 12 April 1895, entry 26, p. 168) and
Ruža (1897).

House 114 is a zadruga — three nuclear families of the descendants of Ilija
Blažević share the number across thirteen years: Toma Blažević and Terezija
Pećanić (1882, 1884), Pavao Blažević and Marija Tomljanović (1890), and Juraj
himself (1895).

2. SENJ, 1915 — the death of ZVONIMIR VUKELIĆ
===========================================================================

ZVONIMIR MATEJ VUKELIĆ, born 13 January 1883, was the first husband of MILKA
LUCIJA PAPIĆ (b. Senj, 9 October 1886) — my great-grandmother. He died in 1915
and left her a widow at twenty-eight, with a daughter of eight and a son of two,
five years before she married Ljubomir Blažević on 8 December 1920.

I know nothing else about him at all.

  2a. The SENJ DEATH REGISTER for 1915, for Zvonimir Vukelić — the date, the
      cause, his occupation and his age.

Their MARRIAGE I already hold: Senj marriages p. 294, no. 30, 4 November 1907.
So the death falls between November 1907 and December 1920, and the family
account puts it in 1915. If 1915 yields nothing, that bracket is the range.


===========================================================================
3. A household that stood godparent twice and is in no tree
===========================================================================

Reading the godparent column of the Sv. Jakov Krmpote baptisms — a column I had
never read — turned up JANDRE BUTORAC and his wife MARIJA standing twice for
children of Toma Blažević at Smokvica house 114:

    25 July 1882   Ivan Blažević — "Pere ?ećanić i Marija Butorac, supruga
                   Jandrina"
    25 October 1884  Antonija "Tonka" Blažević — "Jandre i Marija Butorac,
                   supruzi, rimo-kat. seljaci"

Standing twice, two years apart, is a standing relationship between two
households — and Tonka is a direct ancestor of mine. Neither Jandre nor Marija
exists anywhere in my records.

  3a. The MARRIAGE of a JANDRE (Andrija) BUTORAC at Sv. Jakov Krmpote, roughly
      1870-1882 — with the HOUSE NUMBER of his household. If it sits near 114
      the tie is neighbourhood; if it sits far off, it is much more likely kin.

  3b. In the 1882 entry above, the godfather's surname is illegible in my copy —
      it reads "Pere ?ećanić". If it is PEĆANIĆ he is the child's mother's own
      kin. I would rather be told what the register says than guess.

And one class of book, if any survives
===========================================================================

A STATUS ANIMARUM for Krivi Put, Sv. Jakov Krmpote, Smokvica Krmpotska or Senj
itself would answer more than several individual entries, because it lists a
household together.

I am not asking blind: a page of one survives in my own family papers, captioned
"Our Lady of the Snow, Krivi Put", running 1897 to 1939, with each person entered
as ž. (wife), s. (son), k. (daughter) or i. (child of) beneath the head of the
household. So the books were kept in these parishes.

For Senj there is a specific question attached. I have read Senj baptisms and
marriages and found that this town's entries record NO HOUSE NUMBER at all,
where the village books give one without hesitation. A Senj status animarum
would tell me whether the households were numbered and simply not carried into
the registers.
