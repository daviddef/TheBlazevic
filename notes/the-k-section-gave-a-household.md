# We went to page 34 for one woman. The K section gave a household.

**22 September 2026.** The death index of Kranj–Šmartin for 1812–1835 (NŠAL sig. **04634**) has an
appendix at the back — *«Dostavek ženskih imen za leta 1812–1822»*, a supplement of women's names —
and under **1816** it reads **«Kozina Maria — 34»**. Martin Kozina's grandmother, **Maria «Mina»
Dortschen**, was the last person on the Carniolan line without a death. Page 34 was the obvious
place to go.

**She is not on it. Two other women of her name are.**

## Page 34, which is image 18

The 1812–1835 death register (sig. **00894**) runs **two book pages to one scan** — image 35 is
pages 68 and 69 — so **page 34 is the left leaf of image 18**, and the page number is written on it
in red. The year 1816 stands at the head of the first column.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Den 27 [Februar 1816]** · «Z[ubern]» 4 | **MARIA KOZINAR**, *Häubl.* · weiblich · **ALTER 78 Jahre** · *Altersschwäche* |
| **Den 31 [März 1816]** · Oberfeichting 9 | **MARIA KOZINAR**, *Häubl.* · weiblich · **ALTER 3/4 Jahr** · *Fraiß* |

Maria Dortschen was **twenty at her wedding in October 1800**, so in 1816 she was thirty-six.
A woman of seventy-eight and a baby of nine months are neither of them her.

**The index was not wrong.** It carries **two** «Kozina Maria — 34» lines, one directly under the
other. The first reading of that index had taken one line and stopped.

## The village on the 78-year-old's row is not read

At nine times magnification it is written the same way twice on this page, house 4 both times, and
it reads **«Zubern»** or **«Luberan»**. It is recorded as the clerk wrote it rather than resolved
into a modern name. **What matters for the argument is only that it is not Feichting**, and it is
not.

## The nine-month-old is Maria Dortschen's own daughter

The **baptismal** index for 1812–1831 (sig. **04631**, image 169) lists **«Kosina Maria — 44»**
under 1815. Book page 44 — image 23 of sig. **00885** — reads:

> **den 8ten [August 1815] · Haus 10 · MARIA** · katholisch · weiblich · **ehelich** ·
> Vater: **FRANZ KOSINAR**, *Häubler* · Mutter: **MARIA DORTSCHEN** · *um 10 Uhr Nachmittag*

Born 8 August 1815, dead on 31 March 1816. **Seven and a half months, which is what three quarters
of a year means in that column.** The woman we went looking for was not on page 34. **Her daughter
was.**

| Kosina Valentin — 72 (1820) | p. 72, image 37 | **29 February 1820**, Oberfeichting 9, *Häubler*, **1 Woche**, *Fraiß* |

**The index is wrong once.** Margareth is on page **130**, one opening before the page it promises.
Every other reference in the section landed exactly. An index that is right fifteen times and wrong
once is still the fastest way in — **but the page number is a hint and not a fact.**

## What the six change

**1. Matthäus was Franz's brother, not his father.** He stood witness at Franz's marriage in 1800
and **signed with a cross**, and this archive wrote for a day that *nothing here says which*. His
death says which: **45 in 1819 is born about 1774**, and Franz was born 1775–76. Two men born a
year apart are not father and son. **He was twenty-six when he stood up for his brother, and he
could not write his own name.**

**2. Franz kept Oberfeichting 9 himself.** The baptism of **Valentin**, book p. 108 (sig. 00885,
image 55), reads **«Den 15ten Hornung 1820 · Ober-Feichting · 9 · VALENTIN · ehelich · Vater:
FRANZ KOSINA, *Häubler* · Mutter: MARINA DORTSCHEN»**. He was buried on the 29th — 1820 was a leap
year — aged one week. **It is the only record that puts Franz at that house as the householder**,
twenty-one years before his grandson Martin was born in it and twenty-nine before he died in it as
an *Ausnehmer*. The house was never only his son Johann's.

**3. Three of the six known children did not live.** Elisabeth five months, Maria nine months,
Valentin fourteen days, in 1813, 1816 and 1820. **Gertraud, born between two of them in 1814, lived
to bear Martin in 1841**; Johann, born about 1802, kept the house; and a **second Maria**, baptised
6 December 1818, was named for the sister who had died two years before. That is not a remarkable
household; it is an ordinary one, and it is the first time this archive has been able to say so
about anybody in Carniola.

> **This paragraph said FIVE for part of the same evening**, on a count that listed five and wrote
> six. The sixth is real and is the 1818 Maria, found in the baptismal index an hour later — see the
> addendum below. **Corrected here rather than left to contradict its own note.**

**4. The surname is Dortschen.** Six clerks across thirty-three years: **«Dortshen»** 1800 ·
**«Dortschen»** 1812 · **«Do[m]shan»** 1814 · **«Dortschen»** 1815 · **«Dortschen»** 1820 ·
**«Tom[sh]shan»** 1833. **Four of the six read Dortschen**, and the two that do not are this
archive's own bracketed readings of a hand it found hard. The lead spelling on the site changed
that day, and all six are kept.

**5. Margaretha and the elder Maria are kin without a proof.** Margaretha died **unmarried at
forty-five in Franz's own house** — born about 1781, of his generation, most likely his sister. The
elder Maria was born about **1738**, a generation above him, and is a **candidate for his mother**.
Neither entry names a parent. **They are recorded as what they are and no more.**

## Where Maria Dortschen's death is not

**The search is now exhaustive for this parish**, which is worth more than another guess.

* **1812–1835** (sig. 04634) — the six above. **Not her.**
* **1836–1869** (sig. 04635) — Franz 1849 and Luzia 1850, and nobody else. **Not her.**

She was alive in **February 1820**, at about forty, because Valentin was baptised then and entered
*ehelich*. So she died **unindexed, after 1869 at eighty-nine or more, or in another parish**.

**Next, in the same free books:**

1. **Deaths 1870–1890** (sig. 03916) and **1890–1911** (sig. 03917).
2. The baptisms still unread in the 1812–1831 K section: **Kosina Dorothea, p. 8 (1813)**,
   **Kosina Marianna, p. 16 (1813)** and a second **Kosina Maria, p. 84 (1818)**. Two of those fall
   in the same year and may be one household or two.
3. **Matthäus at Oberfeichting 14** — a different house from Franz's 9, and his own marriage and
   children are not looked for.

## Method, for the sister archives

**Do not read the one page an index sends you to. Read the whole letter.** This sitting was asked
for a single entry on a single page. Reading the section around it cost four more downloads and
turned one negative into **six people, a house, a corrected kinship and a corrected spelling** —
and the negative it was asked for got *stronger*, not weaker, because it became exhaustive instead
of partial.

**And read the index's own appendices.** The women of 1812–1822 are in a supplement at the back of
this one, under a Slovene heading, **not in the main alphabetical run**. A K section read without
it would have missed both Marias and Elisabeth — three of the six.

## Added the same evening: the late registers, and what they actually answered

**The two books after 1869 are not indexed**, so they were walked. Deaths **1890–1911** (sig. 03917),
**199 openings**, plus the 1887–1890 tail of sig. 03916 — every *Ime, priimek in stan mrličev* column,
read five openings to a sheet.

**There is not one Kozina death in this parish in twenty-five years.**

> **CORRECTED LATER THE SAME EVENING.** The section below concluded that Gertraud **left the
> parish**. She did not. The volume *before* the one that had been read — deaths **1870–1890** — has
> her on **book page 69**: **«5. — 7. Marec 1880 · Zg. Bitno · 9 · JERA KOZINA, neomožena gostja ·
> ALTER 65 let · božjast in jetika»**. *Jera* is the Slovene for Gertruda; she was baptised 18 March
> 1814 and died **thirteen days short of sixty-six**, unmarried, **in the house she bore Martin in**.
> The reasoning below — no dagger in 1888, therefore living in 1888 — was wrong. **An absent mark is
> not a record.** The struck-through argument is kept where it was made.

### Which settles Gertraud, who was not the one we were looking for

Martin's mother was **alive in January 1888**: her son's Senj marriage carries **no dagger** against her
name and calls her a **posjednica**, a property-owner. The index for 1836–1869 holds only Franz and
Luzia. And 1887–1911 holds nobody of the name at all.

**Alive in 1888, and not dead at Kranj–Šmartin afterwards. She left.**

**The search for her moves to SENJ** — where her son was a *trgovac*, where she is named in his marriage
entry, and where this archive already reads registers. She has **one record in the world**; this would be
her second.

### One Kozina woman is in the book, and only as a mother

**Marija, born Kozina**, married **Kancijan Česen**, a *hišar-dninar* at **Stražišče 84** — the parish
seat, not Bitnje. Two of their children die: **Kancijan** 1892, aged one and three quarter months, and
**Pavla** 1893, aged two. Their ages put her birth about **1860–1870**.

**Nothing joins her to this family but the surname and the parish.** The document that would settle it is
her own marriage, in **sig. 03919 (1881–1899)**, which names both parents of both parties. Not opened.

### And a sixth child, from the baptismal index

**«Kosina Maria — 84»** under 1818 is **Franz Kosinar, Häusler** and **Maria Dortschan** again, on
**6 December 1818** — **named for the daughter who had died in March 1816**. Re-using a dead child's name
is ordinary and is not a clerk's slip.

**So the marriage produced six known children, not five**, and three of the six died under a year. This
note said five for part of one evening, on a count that listed five and wrote six; the sixth then turned
out to be a real and different child.

### Two index entries that do not land

**«Kosina Dorothea — 8»** and **«Kosina Marianna — 16»**, both 1813. Both pages were opened and the whole
*Namen des Vaters* column of each read at 2.4×: **there is no Kosina on either.** The Dorothea on page 8
is the daughter of *Anton Polzner, Inwohner*, at house 12.

This is the same index that sends a reader to **p.132** for a Margareth who is on **p.130**. So these are
**misdirections, not errors** — the children are somewhere in the volume and the page numbers do not
reach them. Left open.

### What the walk cost, for the sister archives

**337 openings downloaded, 44 sheet-reads, one evening.** Crop the one column that carries the surname,
tile **five openings to a sheet**, and read the sheet. Do not read pages. **And pick the window from what
you already know**: the marriage entry that said *posjednica* with no dagger is what made 1888 the floor,
and it is why twenty-five years was enough rather than a hundred.

## And then the volume before it, which had the answer all along

**337 openings across the two post-1869 registers, and exactly one Kozina death in either.**

> **5. — 7. [Marec 1880] · Zg. Bitno · 9 · JERA KOZINA, *neomožena gostja* · katolik · ženski ·
> ALTER 65 let · *božjast in jetika* · M. Žarnik**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Jera** | the Slovene short form of **Gertruda** — the book is in Slovene by 1880 |
| **65 let** | baptised **18 March 1814**, dead **5 March 1880**: thirteen days short of sixty-six, so the age is **exact**, not rounded |
| **Zg. Bitno 9** | Zgornje Bitno is **Oberfeichting**. House 9 is her father's in 1820, her brother's from 1833, where her father died in 1849 — **and where she bore Martin in 1841** |
| **neomožena** | **never married**, which the *unehelich* on Martin's baptism had said thirty-nine years earlier |
| **gostja** | a **lodger**. She lived her life in her brother's house |
| **božjast in jetika** | the falling sickness and consumption — epilepsy and tuberculosis |

**Martin's mother now has a birth and a death, both read at the page.** She is the only person above
him of whom that is true.

### What I got wrong, and the rule that comes out of it

Two hours before this was found, this archive published that **Gertraud had left the parish**. The
argument: her son's Senj marriage of January 1888 carries **no dagger** against her name, so she was
living; and she is in no Kranj–Šmartin death after 1887.

**She had been dead eight years.** The 1888 clerk simply did not mark her.

> **An absent mark is not a record.** A dagger present is evidence. A dagger absent is a clerk's
> habit, a hurried entry, or a groom who did not say. **Never build a date on something that is not
> written down.**

### And a second index turns out to be incomplete

**Johann** was alive in **1856**, when his daughter Ana was baptised. He is in **neither** post-1869
register. So he died **between 1857 and 1869** — **and the letter-tabbed index for 1836–1869 does
not list him.** It holds two Kozina and should hold at least three.

That index is the only authority this archive has for saying **Maria Dortschen** is not in those
years. **It is not good enough for that.** The register itself — sig. **00895**, about two hundred
openings — has to be walked, exactly as these two were.

### The cost, for the sister archives

**337 openings, 44 sheet-reads, one evening, one find.** The method that worked: crop the single
column carrying the surname, tile **five openings to a sheet**, read the sheet, and never open a
page until a sheet says to. **And when a targeted window gives nothing, widen it before concluding
anything** — the answer here was in the volume nobody had planned to read.
