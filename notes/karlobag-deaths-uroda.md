# The Karlobag death register, opened — and what it says about the Urode

14 September 2026. Opened to answer one question: **when did ahnentafel 54,
Joannis Georgius Uroda, born 22 April 1777, die?** The census of 1844 had no
household headed by a man of his age, and the archive had no death date for him
at all.

---

## The book, and the thing that makes it searchable

Karlobag deaths 1804–1861 sit on the second Karlobag film, images ~118 to ~165.
The register is **Latin prose** to about 1830 and **tabular** after —
*Anno, Mensis, Dies | Nomen | Cognomen | Ætas | Morbus | Sacramenta | Locus
sepulturæ* — though the clerk kept writing prose inside the ruling. By **1854**
the headings are in Croatian (*Godine 1854*).

**And there is an alphabetical index**, images ~168–192, with a printed letter at
the head of each column: **Cognomen et Nomen | dies | mensis | annus | folium.**

That index is the find. It turns a fifty-seven-year register into a lookup, and
this archive did not know it existed. Its folio numbers were spot-checked twice
and were right both times.

## Ahnentafel 55, confirmed to the year — and her husband's trade

**Folio 52, Anno Domini 1834, Die 6ª Aug.**

> **Maria uxor piscatoris Georgii Uroda annorum 45**, in communione S. Matris
> Ecclesiæ… die 7ª hujus sepultum est Carlobagi in cœmeterio communi, confessa
> mihi [Carolo] Perxevich… sanctissimoque Viatico refecta, et Sacri Olei
> Unctione roborata per Eumdem.

Three things at once.

**The date is the tree's exactly** — 6 August 1834 for ahnentafel 55.
**The age is the tree's exactly** — *annorum 45*, born about 1789, and her
baptism was read in **August 1789**.
**And she is *uxor*, not *vidua*.** Her husband was **alive** on 6 August 1834.

That single word does the work of an afternoon: it **rules out** the Georgius
Uroda who died in 1808 and the Georgius Uroda who died in **1817**, and it leaves
exactly one Georgius after 1834.

It also gives him a trade the archive did not have. **Piscator — a fisherman.**

## Ahnentafel 54, on the balance of the register

**Folio 66, Anno Domini 1846, Die 11ª Januarii**

> **Georgius Uroda annorum 72, piscator**, in communione… sepultus est in
> cœmeterio communi, absolutus, et Sacramentis…

**Same name, same trade, the only Georgius Uroda to die after his wife.** Aged 72
in January 1846 gives a birth about **1774**; the tree says **22 April 1777**,
which would make him 68.

**Three years out, and this archive is not calling that a match.** A stated age
in this register is sometimes exact to the year — *annorum 45* for his wife was —
and sometimes not. What would settle it is the **marriage register 1804–1861**,
on the same film and still unopened, which would name his father and fix his age
at marriage.

The other candidate was read and is worse: **Joannes Uroda, *civis*, annorum 59**,
died 8 December 1843 — born about 1784, seven years out, and a **citizen**
rather than a fisherman.

## What follows, if the 1846 man is him

It makes the census household legible. **Uroda Maria vid., born 18 March 1789**,
heads a household at image 64 of the *Stanje duša* with **Ive (1811), Vicko
(1816) and Kate Maria (1820)** — and those three bracket **ahnentafel 27**, born
23 April 1810.

Read together: Georgius the fisherman married Maria Gerkacs, had Matija, Ive,
Vicko and Kate Maria between 1810 and 1820, lost his wife in 1834, **married a
second Maria**, died in 1846 — and his widow was left keeping his grown children
when the census was taken.

**That is a reconstruction, not a reading.** The second marriage is inferred from
two women of the same name and the same birth year, and the marriage register
would prove or break it in one entry.

## Method, recorded

Two deaths were read at the page rather than taken from the index, because an
index is a finding aid and not a record. Both agreed with it. And the archive's
own rule earned its keep again: the **1817** Georgius was the obvious answer,
the one a hurried reader would have taken — and one word on a different page,
*uxor*, killed it.
