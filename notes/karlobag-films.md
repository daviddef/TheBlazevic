# What the Karlobag films actually hold

14 September 2026. Karlobag is the best-read parish in this archive — twelve
baptisms out of it, more than any other — and until today **nobody had ever
checked what years its films cover**. The parish table carried `unknown` in all
three columns, and `/tree` printed that as "ranges not checked" against four
walls.

All three films were opened. **The catalogue label holds**, which is worth
saying plainly because at Krmpote it did not.

## Three things this changes

**1. Four walls stop being «not known» and become «go and read it».**

| Ahn | Who | What is in range |
|---|---|---|
| 104 | Jacob Pilipić | a baptism of about 1741 — inside 1691-1770 |
| 108 | Georgi Uroda | a baptism of about 1750 — inside |
| 210 | Vicentius Luckinić | a baptism of about 1700 — inside, and only just |
| 212 | Josiph Smojver Vukić | **his own baptism, 1731** — inside |

**2. And a fifth, which is a different kind of win.** *Maria Uroda* (ahn 109)
is on the chart under **her husband's surname**, which is the tree's convention
and not a record. You cannot search for a surname you do not have — but her
**marriage of about 1775 is inside the filmed register**, and a marriage entry
names the bride's father. That wall is answerable.

*Maria Luckinić* (ahn 211) is not: her marriage falls about **1725**, and the
Karlobag marriage book on film begins in **1736**. Eleven years.

**3. There is a household census for Karlobag and this archive did not know.**
122 images, by **house number**, with ages and a communion column for every year
from 1844. The Pilipići and the Urode are Karlobag families of exactly that
period. This is the same class of instrument as the Otočac *Stanje duša* — and
unlike Otočac's, nobody has even opened it.

## The caution, carried forward

The leaves at the front of film 1 are **not in chronological order**: image 4 is
1723 and image 6 is the 1690s, both stamped with the same folio number. Do not
infer a date from an image number on this film without reading the page.

And the language changes under you: **Croatian prose to the 1750s, Latin prose
after, Latin tabular from about 1780**. The archive already knew one Karlobag
volume was in Croatian. It is the earlier half of two of them.
