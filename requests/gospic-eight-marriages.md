# Državni arhiv u Gospiću — eight marriages that would rebuild a family

**To:** Državni arhiv u Gospiću, Kaniška 17, 53000 Gospić
**zahtjevi@arhiv-gospic.hr**

**Status: drafted, not sent.** This replaces an earlier draft that asked for
thirty-four people. Asking for eight is a better question, and this explains why.

## What is actually wrong with our records

We hold **1,245 people** across nine surnames of the Senj–Otočac district. Last
week we measured something we had never measured: how many of them are connected
to our subject, **Hedviga Blažević of Senj (1926–2001)**, by *documented*
links — parents, children, marriages — rather than by notes a researcher once
typed into the tree where an ancestor should be.

**The answer was 393. Thirty-two per cent.**

But the remaining 852 are not scattered. Removing those notes does not dissolve
the tree into individuals: it breaks it into **92 intact families**. Each has its
own parents, children, marriages and house numbers. Each is simply **not joined
to ours**, and the join is missing at one place — the oldest generation.

**So we are not missing eight hundred documents. We are missing about eight.**

## The three we would ask for first

1. **Nicolai Žubrinić, born 1769.** Two hundred and seventeen people hang below
   him — larger than most whole families on our site, and connected to us by
   nothing at all. His marriage would fall about **1787–1799**.
2. **Josephus Žubrinić, born 1782, at *Prozor 46*.** Eight children. A village
   *and* a house number, so the entry can be found without a name index.
3. **Domianus Perpić, born 1820, at *Krivi Put 136*** — a house we already work
   on, and thirty-nine Prpići and Perpići follow him.

## Why a marriage and not a baptism

A baptism names the child and its parents. **A marriage names both parties and
both sets of parents** — four names and usually two house numbers in one act. It
is the only entry that can join two households at once, which is precisely the
shape of what we are missing.
