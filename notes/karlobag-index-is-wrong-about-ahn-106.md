# The Karlobag index names the wrong mother for a direct ancestor

*Work list row 54, 20 September 2026. Found while searching the record index
for the walls; recorded because it cuts against the index, not for it.*

---

## What the index says

FamilySearch's *Croatia, Church Books* index, searched at Karlobag:

| Date | Child | Father | Mother |
|---|---|---|---|
| 18 December 1752 | Barbaru Smoiver | Osipa | **Antonie** |
| **18 January 1754** | **Vicencza Fabiana Smoiver** | **Osipa** | **Marie** |
| 9 July 1764 | Margaritham Smoiver | Josephi | **Antoniæ** |

`1:1:QKMX-BSQ4` is the 1754 entry. **Vicencza Fabiana Smoiver is ahnentafel
106**, and the tree gives his mother as **ahnentafel 213, Antonia Pekass**.

Read from the index alone, that is a conflict on the direct line — a mother
called Marie between two children of Antonia, twelve years apart.

## What the register says

It is not a conflict. **This archive read the entry at full resolution on 10
September 2026**, and the book is kept in Croatian rather than Latin:

> **Dan 18: detto.** …*Isti dan kerstih **vicencia Fabiana sina Ospa y Antonie
> Smoiver**, [d]iku kumi Petar y Mande…*

***sina Ospa y Antonie*** — **son of Osip and Antonia.** The register names
Antonia. **The index is simply wrong**, and the tree is right.

`notes/reading-2026-09-10.md` also records a detail the index does not carry and
which may be how the error arose: **two boys named Vicenac Fabijan were baptised
at Karlobag that same day**, the other a son of *Matija Kriškovich*. Ours is the
second, *isti dan*.

## Why it is written down

Row 54 turned on the discovery that **the Karlobag baptisms are indexed**, which
made four walls into lookups instead of a walk through 327 images. That is still
true and it is still the right instrument. This is the other half of it:

**An index is a transcription, and a transcription of this hand is wrong often
enough to matter.** The same sitting has already recorded that it writes Pilipić
as *Pidepics* and *Pilleppics* and Gerkacs as *Gergacs, Gergocz, Geraecs* and
*Gergich*. Those are spellings, and a spelling only hides a person. **This one
does something worse: it names the wrong woman, legibly, on a direct ancestor.**

**So the index finds the page and the page settles the fact**, and where the two
disagree the image wins. This archive would have published a contradiction about
ahnentafel 213 if it had trusted the search box over its own reading — and it
came within one query of doing so.
