# The Senj cemetery, photographed October 2024, read September 2026

15 September 2026. **One hundred and five photographs taken on 9 October 2024
had never been read.** They are the second half of a trip whose first half —
[the Otočac churchyard](../sources/graves/otocac-2024-10-08.psv) — was recorded
a day earlier and written up. The GPS on the files puts every one of them in a
400-metre box at **44.985 N, 14.924 E**, between 02:37 and 04:22 UTC: the Senj
municipal cemetery, in one sweep of about an hour and three quarters.

The Otočac note ended on an honest negative: *"No stone names anyone already in
the tree, and that is the honest headline."* **Senj is the opposite.**

## The pattern in the errors

**Every death year on these five stones is right. Two of the five birth years
are one out**, and both in the same direction — the stone is a year earlier than
the register. Ljubomir 1891 for 1892; Antonija 1860 for 1859.

The archive already knew about Ljubomir's. **It is not a one-off.** A death is
carved by people who have just lived through it; a birth is carved from memory,
sometimes forty years late. **On this evidence, a Senj stone is good evidence
for a death year and weak evidence for a birth year** — and that is a rule worth
having before the next stone is read.

## A name that is not in the tree

**MATILDA PAPIĆ ✽1895 †1921**, the third name on her parents' cross, dead at
twenty-six. The tree has **no Matilda**. It does have two Papić daughters born
in 1895 — **Franka**, 20 January, and **Sulka**, 7 February — and **neither has
a death recorded**. One of them is probably this woman under a name the family
used. It is not being decided here; it is in `sources/found.psv` as an open
question with a date attached.

## And a surname that is not in this archive at all

**IVAN DEFRANĆESKI 1925–1995**, on a polished slab with raised metal letters,
photographed five times. **The surname does not occur once in this archive's
tree.** It is recorded in the survey because it was photographed in the survey,
and flagged for the Defranceski archive rather than joined to anything here.

## Three by-names, carved

The Otočac trip produced one: *OBITELJI **ŽUBRINIĆ-ČUN** i ŽANIĆ*. Senj produces
three more of the same kind, and three double-surname heads besides:

* **TIHI DOM OBITELJ VUKELIĆ — PURICA**
* **MILAN VUKELIĆ –KOŽUN–**
* **KRUNOSLAV PRPIĆ / PECE**

and *PAPIĆ-GLAVIČIĆ*, *OBITELJ PRPIĆ -BORAS-*, *OBITELJI BORAS - MILKOVIĆ*. The
practice the parish registers show with *aliter* and *vulgò* in the 1700s is
still being cut into granite in the 1990s.

## One line that is a piece of history rather than genealogy

On the Papić Stjepan grave, a separate plate:

> **LEO LONČARIĆ ✽1920 †1945 · BLAJBURG**

The place of death is carved on the stone. That is the Bleiburg repatriations of
May 1945, and the family put it in writing on a headstone in a country where,
for forty-five years afterwards, it was not written down anywhere else.

## What was not read

**IMG_8071** is a fallen plaque under reeds, upside down, reading *«MARIJAN …»*
and no more. It is **the only name in the 105 that could not be got at all**, and
it is worth a torch and ten minutes on the next visit. Four frames are cemetery
views, a dozen are blank plates and accidental shots, and seven are people
standing at the graves.

## How the reading was done, and what it is worth

**190 name-rows are now in `sources/graves/senj-2024-10-09.psv`.** Each carries a
confidence letter, and the letters mean something specific:

* **r** — cropped and enlarged **3× to 8×** and read at full resolution. Every
  ancestor row is an **r**.
* **p** — read from a **3×3 contact sheet** at about a third scale. The surname
  is secure; **the digits are not verified** and want a full-resolution pass.
* **?** — uncertain, not to be relied on.

The contact sheets were a triage, not a shortcut: they decided *which* stones to
open at full resolution, and no **r** was claimed without one. That distinction
is the whole reason the confidence column exists.

**Before today `graves.json` held 72 memorials, every one of them a Find a Grave
transcription from Australia or Canada, and not a single Croatian cemetery.**
These 190 are read off photographs of the stones themselves, which is better
evidence — and for Senj it is the only evidence there is, because Find a Grave
has never covered it.
