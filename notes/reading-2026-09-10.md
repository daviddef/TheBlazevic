## Karlobag baptisms, April 1777 — Joannes Georgius Uroda (ahn 54)

> Ego Joës Ant[oniu]s Ivankovich Parochus Loci baptizavi Infantem **heri natum**
> ex **Georgio, et Maria Uroda** Conjugibus hujus Loci, cui impositum est
> **Nomen Joannis Georgij**. Patrini fuerunt Dnus Pro Colonellus **Joannes
> Hallinich de Zirfeld**, et [tit.] Dna Maria Conjux Dni Centurionis **Danielis
> de Hofenegg**.

Confirms year, parish and both parents, and that he was born the day before the
baptism. The page carries **no day number** — 22 April comes from the index, not
from the register.

Two things worth keeping:

1. **The priest wrote the genitive.** *Nomen Joannis Georgij*, not *Joannes
   Georgius*. The case ending in the tree is not a modern transcription error;
   it is copied faithfully from a register that used the oblique form.
2. **The godparents are Frontier officers** — a Pro-Colonel and a Captain's
   wife, standing for the child of a Karlobag family. Consistent with the
   *Dominus* / *negotiator* milieu already documented for Karlobag.

## Vrbnik baptisms, 14 March 1806 — Josephum Antić (ahn 24), house 244

> Die 14. Martii Ao 1806. baptizavi ego **Michaël Kombol** C.L. infantem lege
> natum patre **Joanne**, Matre **Marthæ Antich <u>Papa</u>**, **nomine
> Josephum**, susceptoribus conjugibus **Judice Mathia Jellichich** et ejus
> consorte Margarita.

**The tree's date, 14 March 1806, is exact.** The father is Joannes — ahnentafel
48, alive and named in 1806, consistent with his own dates of 1765–1829.

The godparent is the **village judge**. And every child named on this page is in
the accusative — *Mariam, Joannem, Matthæam, Magdalenam, Vincentium, Nicolaum*.
Kombol wrote *nomine* + accusative as a fixed formula.

**The scribe underlines house by-names.** *Antich <u>Papa</u>*, *Loncsarich
<u>Franich</u>*, *Antich <u>Dudin</u>*, *Pobor <u>Rataj</u>*. These are
**nadimci** — they distinguish households where a village shares one surname,
doing the same work as the house numbers at Smokvica and Krivi Put. Which puts a
documented *Papa* next to the 79 people this tree writes **Papić (Papa)**, and
raises a question this archive should not answer yet: whether that is the
by-name of an *Antić* household rather than a surname of its own.

| | |
|---|---|

| Bride | **Franjka Papić**, *radnica u tvornici duhana* — tobacco factory worker. Senj, living Senj. b. **20 January 1895**, unmarried |

| Witnesses | **Mile Prpić**, *gostioničar* (innkeeper) · **Zvonko M. Vukelić**, *pivar* (brewer) |

Three things:

**A Papić marries a Prpić again.** Franjka's own mother is Tonka r. Prpić of
Krivi Put; Franjka marries Mile Prpić of Krivi Put. The same two villages pair
in consecutive generations.

**Her father is a day-labourer.** *Nadničar* — the lowest occupational label in
the book. Set against the Karlobag *Dominus* and *negotiator* of a century
earlier, the direction of travel is downward, and matches what the railway did
to Senj's port.

**Zvonko M. Vukelić is a witness.** Zvonko is the short form of Zvonimir, and
the middle initial matches **Zvonimir *Matei* Vukelić** — Milka's first husband,
married 1907. He is standing witness at his sister-in-law's wedding in August
1914, described as a **brewer**. The tree gives his occupation as *gostioničar,
bankar*. This is the first document found so far that places him inside the
family other than his own marriage record — independent corroboration of a
marriage that had left almost no trace.

### And a contradiction that follows from reading both

Franjka's register says **20 January 1895**. Šulka's says **7 February 1895**.
Both are daughters of **Josip Papić and Antonija r. Prpić**, and each date is
carried by that woman's own marriage entry, eighteen days apart.

They cannot both be right for full sisters, and neither is a guess by this
archive — both are written in the register. Possibilities, none preferred:

- one bride's stated date is wrong (a marriage entry records what the bride
  said, not what the baptism book says);
- they are twins and the dates are garbled;
- one of them belongs to a different Papić couple — there are at least three
  Papić households having children in Senj in these years.

**What would close it:** the **Senj baptism register for January–February 1895**.
Both entries would be within three weeks of each other in the same book.

## Otočac 1834 and 1845 — the Orešković / Marković contradiction, resolved

### The 1834 entry

> **1834 Januarii die 24a Ottocsitii** · **Nicolaus** filius legitimus Catholicus ·
> **Confiniarius, ex Pago Shumechicza, N. 24** · **Michaël Xubrinich, et Maria
> uxor ejus nata Oreskovich**, Catholici · Patrini: **Petrus filius Francisci
> Xubrinich, Vigiliarum Ma[gis]ter pensionatus**, et **Maria uxor Joannis
> Xubrinich, Sylvarum Custos in Shumechicza**

### The 1845 entry

> **1845 Martii 16ta Ottocii** · **Josephus** filius legitimus Romano-Catholicus ·
> **Confiniarius** · **Michaël, et Maria Žubrinić, nata Marković** ·
> Patrini: **Vitus Kranjčević Murarius**, et **Maria Žubrinić, mulier Custodis
> Carcerum**

### What this settles

**Already known, now read at source.** That Maria Catharina is the wrong woman was
established earlier from her own 1835 baptism (Dubrava N° 29, parents Marcus
Orešković *adoptatus Sekula* and Lucia née Sekula) and is set out on the
Frontier page. Reading the 1834 entry directly confirms it from the other side:

**Maria Catharina Orešković, born 8 September 1835, cannot be the mother of
Nicolaus, baptised 24 January 1834.** The 1834 entry describes a married woman
bearing a *legitimate* child nineteen months before Maria Catharina was born.
The tree has merged **two different women** who share a surname:

- **Maria née Orešković**, wife of Michaël Žubrinić, bearing children from 1834
  — necessarily born somewhere around 1810–15, and otherwise undocumented here;
- **Maria Catharina Orešković, b. 8 September 1835**, daughter of Marcus
  Orešković and Lucia Sekula — a real person, with her own baptism scan, who is
  simply not this child's mother.

This is not a date to be corrected. It is a **join to be broken**, and it takes
a branch with it: ahnentafel 21's entire ancestry — Marcus Orešković (42), Lucia
Sekula (43) and everything above them — hangs off the wrong woman.

### What it does not settle

By 1845 Michaël Žubrinić's wife is **Maria née Marković**. Either the first
Maria died and he remarried a second Maria, or there are two men of the same
name — Žubrinić has 68 records at Otočac, so that is not far-fetched.

**Nothing here chooses between those.** What would: the **Otočac marriage
register**, for a Žubrinić–Marković marriage between 1834 and 1845, and the
**death register** for a Maria Žubrinić née Orešković in the same window.

### A correction to my own first reading

I first read the entry above this one — 11 March 1845, *Gregorius* — as a second
Michaël **Žubrinić** with a third wife, and took it as proof that the name was
duplicated in the parish. It is not. The register's convention is *[husband] et
[wife] **SURNAME** nata [maiden name]*, so that entry reads **Michaël et
Magdalena Marković nata Kostelac** — a **Marković** family. Recorded because the
misreading would have been an attractive piece of evidence and it was wrong.

### And three more Frontier offices

The godparents in these two entries hold, between them: **Vigiliarum Magister
pensionatus** (retired watch-master), **Sylvarum Custos** (forest guard) at
Šumećica, and **Custos Carcerum** (keeper of the gaol) — the last two both
Žubrinići. Everyone in both entries is *Confiniarius*.

### This is the best evidence yet on question 4d

The tree gives **one** couple — Michael "Mate" Žubrinić and Maria Catharina
Orešković — two sons: **Nicolaus, b. 24 January 1834**, and **Nikola, b. 23
August 1880**. Forty-six years apart, which /frontier already flagged as a sign
that something had slipped.

This register confirms the 1880 son independently, and names his parents in the
same words: **Mate Žubrinić and Marija née Orešković**. So both sons really do
have parents of those names.

**Therefore there are two couples of the same two names, a generation apart, and
the tree has collapsed them into one.** And the dates decide which is which:

- **Couple A** — Michaël Žubrinić + Maria née Orešković, *Confiniarius* at
  Šumećica 24, son **Nicolaus 1834**. Maria must have been born around 1810–15.
- **Couple B** — Mate Žubrinić + Marija née Orešković, *poljodjelci* at Otočac,
  son **Nikola 1880**, both dead by 1910.

**Maria Catharina Orešković, b. 8 September 1835, cannot belong to Couple A —
but she fits Couple B.** Which changes the shape of the error: she is not
attached to the wrong *family*, she is attached to the wrong *generation of the
right family*. That is a much more tractable problem, and it means the
Orešković–Sekula branch above her may be perfectly sound — just hanging one
generation too high.

Two cautions. Nikola born 1880 to a woman born 1835 makes her **forty-five**,
late but not impossible. And nothing yet shows how Couple B joins Couple A —
the obvious guess, that Mate of Couple B is a son of Couple A, is only a guess,
though the tree does list a **Matthæus** among Michael's children.

**And another office.** *Kontrolor gradske daće* — municipal excise controller.
The Žubrinić pattern of salaried office (watch-master, forest guard, gaoler,
forest ranger) carries into twentieth-century civil administration.

# Direct-line documents

## Senj deaths p.225 no.60 — Toma Blažević (ahn 8), 26 July 1907

> **1907 srpnja 26** · **Blažević Toma**, *nadničar* · **Udovac iza pok.
> Tereze** · born **sv. Jakov Krmpote**, living **Senj** · RK · **69 god.** ·
> *Tuberculosis* · *providjen* · buried **sv. Vid, 28 srpnja** · Matija Golik,
> kapelan

The tree has the date and nothing else. The register adds **the cause, the age,
the birth parish, his widowerhood and the cemetery** — and confirms 26 July 1907
exactly. Sixty-nine in July 1907 puts his birth in 1837–38, matching the tree's
27 January 1838.

Two things follow.

**He died in Senj, not at Smokvica.** Born in the parish of sv. Jakov Krmpote,
recorded at **house 114** at his own birth, and dying a *nadničar* — day-labourer
— in the town. The move off the land that the Papići show from Selce, the
Blaževići made from Krmpote, and Toma made it himself rather than leaving it to
his children.

**Tuberculosis, and he was not alone on the page.** Entry 59, five days earlier,
is **Marija Nabrčnik, 30, *radnica u tvornici duhana***, also tuberculosis. Two
of seven deaths on one page of the Senj register, one of them a tobacco-factory
worker.

### And it exposes an impossible date in the tree

The tree gives his wife **Tereza Pećanić (ahn 9)** as ***died before 1850***.
The same tree has her **marrying Toma on 21 November 1858** and bearing children
in **1860, 1880, 1882 and 1884**. She cannot have died eight years before her own
wedding and thirty-four years before her last child.

The register supplies the only bound anyone actually has: Toma is entered
**udovac iza pok. Tereze** — widower of the late Tereza — so **Tereza was dead by
26 July 1907**, and alive in 1884. *Died before 1850* is simply wrong and is
corrected to that span.

## Two disputed readings settled

### *Ballinich* is wrong: the name is **Hallinich de Zirfeld**

The 1777 Uroda godfather was read as *Pro-Colonellus Joannes **Ballinich** de
Zirfeld*. The same surname recurs in a **February 1779** entry on the Bacchi
page — *Ill[ustrissi]mus D[omi]nus Joës **Bapta** / **Hallinich de Zirfeld*** —
and there the words *Joës Bapta* sit on the same line, giving a clear
**two-bowled capital B** for comparison. The surname's initial is a single
looped ascender with a leg: plainly not that letter. **H** (or possibly K), not
**B**.

That February entry is worth its own line: the child is *ex **Antonio Bar. de
Holstein** et Maria* — a **Baron von Holstein** at Karlobag — and the godmother
is the widow of **Wolff Georg de Ruskich**.

## The Latin case, settled by the same priest twice

The <a>name page</a> argues that the oblique endings are the priests' own Latin
rather than a transcriber's error. Karlobag now proves it from a single hand:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| 1777 | *cui impositum est **Nomen Joannis Georgij*** | **genitive** |
| 1781 | *cui impositum est **nomen Oliva Maria*** | **nominative** |

Both written by **Joannes Antonius Ivankovich**, four years apart, in the same
book. A third priest, **Michael Martinaz**, writes *Nomen **Caetani Antonij***
— genitive — in 1779. It is not a system, and it is not one person's mistake: it
is scribal caprice, and the tree inherits whichever case the priest felt like.

## The join it broke: Caetano Bacchi (ahn 214)

The 1779 entry is a real baptism of a real **Caetanus Antonius Bacchi**. It is
almost certainly **not this archive's ahn 214**.

| | |
|---|---|
| ahn 214 | **Caetano Bacchi**, born *"12 January 1779 **(Maybe)**"* — the tree's own hedge |
| ahn 107 | his daughter **Oliva Andreana Bacchi**, died 15 September 1795, no birth date |
| ahn 53 | her daughter **Oliva Maria Smojver**, born **11 March 1781** |

**Two years, for two generations.** A man born in 1779 cannot be grandfather to
a girl born in 1781. And Oliva Andreana died in 1795, so she was born well
before 1781 herself — her father belongs somewhere around **1730–1745**.

So somebody found a Caetano Bacchi baptism at Karlobag, attached it, and marked
it *(Maybe)*. It is the same error shape as
[Maria Catharina Orešković](/open-questions) and Vladimira Baricević: **the
right family, the wrong generation.**

**And it takes two more ancestors with it.** The parents named in that 1779
entry — **Angelo Bacchi (ahn 428)** and **Catharina (ahn 429)** — are in this
pedigree *only* because of it. If the baptism is not his, they are not his
parents. Catharina is also one of the seven women on the direct line with no
surname at all, so nothing else anchors her.

**What would settle it:** the Karlobag baptisms for roughly **1725–1750**, for a
Caetano/Gaetano Bacchi — and the **marriage** of Vicentius Fabianus Smojver and
Oliva Andreana Bacchi, which would name her father outright.

### The check that should have caught it, and did not

`tools/consistency.py` compares a parent's dates with a child's. Here the
generation *between* the two — Oliva Andreana — has **no birth year at all**, so
there was no pair to compare and the contradiction fell straight through. The
tool now also walks **grandparent → undated child → grandchild** and requires at
least 24 years across two generations. In this archive it fires exactly once:
on Caetano Bacchi.
