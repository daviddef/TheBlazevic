# Novi Vinodolski's oldest register is not in Latin

Checked 14 September 2026, following the rule this archive learned at Krmpote:
**open the film before trusting the label.**

## The label holds

The waypoint says *Births (Rođeni) 1650-1784*. The **film's own target** —
photographed at the Croatian State Archives on 15 December 1987 — says:

    ŽUPA NOVI VINODOL · HRVATSKA — CROATIA · MATIČNA KNJIGA
    ROĐENIH  1650 – 1786
    VJENČANIH 1674 – 1786
    UMRLIH    1704 – 1782 · ITEM 2

**463 images.** Two independent statements of the same dates, one of them the
archive's own. That is far better corroboration than Krmpote had, and the
**1650** on `/harvest` stands.

## But the hand is the surprise

Image 9, one of the first written leaves:

> *Na 13 Maja  + Krsti pop Jurij Skohil …*
> *Na 26 Maja  + Krsti pop Mikula …*
> *Na 16 Junija + Krsti pop Juraj Skohil … Henon Dorotheom …*

**This is Croatian, not Latin.** *Krsti pop* — "the priest baptised". Dates are
given as *Na 13 Maja*, "on 13 May", with the year heading the section rather
than repeated per entry.

Every register this archive has read so far — Karlobag, Vrbnik, Selce, Otočac —
is in **Latin** until the 1880s, when Senj and Krmpote switch to Croatian. Novi
Vinodolski is in the vernacular **two hundred years earlier**. The Vinodol
littoral was the heartland of the Glagolitic tradition, where the liturgy and
the parish books were kept in Croatian when everywhere else used Latin, and this
is what that looks like in a baptismal register.

## What it means for the plan

`/harvest` counts **120 reachable events** in this parish — the largest single
harvest outstanding, covering Klenovica and Povile and the whole Kalanj
question. It is still the biggest prize. But:

* **The Latin toolkit does not transfer.** *filius legitimus*, *denatorum*,
  *conjugum*, *adstantibus* — none of it appears. The formula is different and
  so is the vocabulary.
* **The early entries may not name parents at all.** The three read here give a
  date, the priest, and names, with no visible *filius/filia* construction.
  Whether the 1650s book records parentage in a usable way is **not yet known**,
  and it is the first thing to check before planning on those 120 events.
* The **1804–1852 volume** is a separate film and will be in the later, ruled,
  Latin form — which is where Klenovica and Povile in the archive's own date
  range actually sit.
