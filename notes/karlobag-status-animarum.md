# The Karlobag Stanje duša, read — and ahnentafel 13 with her brothers and sisters

14 September 2026. The Karlobag household census was found on film that morning
and had never been opened by anyone in this archive. This is what it is and what
it holds.

---

## What the book is

**122 images**, ŽUPA KARLOBAG, *Status animarum*, HDA Br. 715, DGS 2199337.
Content runs from about image 8 to image 112; images 1–7 are filming targets and
the book's cover, and there is **no index at either end**.

Each opening carries **two tables, one per page**, and each table is:

| Nr. Familiæ | Nomina et Cognomina | Ætas | Confirmati | Confessi et Communicati Anno … |
|---|---|---|---|---|

The **Ætas** column is the reason this book matters: it does not give an age, it
gives a **date of birth to the day**. And the communion columns run **one column
per year** — 1844, 1845, 1846, 1847, 1849, 1850, 1851, 1852, 1853, 1854, 1855,
1856, 1857 — so a household can be watched year by year: who was there, who was
confirmed, who stopped appearing.

**Two corrections to what this archive said yesterday.**

1. **It is not ordered by house number.** The left-hand column is *Numerus
   Familiæ*, a family number, and Karlobag's registers of this period do not use
   house numbers at all — the 1838 baptism, re-read the same day, gives none
   either. The Smokvica and Krivi Put habit of numbering houses is not this
   parish's habit.
2. **It is not one census.** The book runs **roughly alphabetically by surname
   twice**: B at image 10, D at 20, N at 44, P at 45–52, T at 80 — and then
   **B again at 82**, M at 95–98, T at 102. The second sequence carries dates
   into the **1870s**. Two censuses, a generation apart, bound as one volume.

## The household

**Image 48, right-hand page, first family.** Written *Pilepich* — the parish's
own spelling of Pilipić.

| Joso f. | 14 Oct. 835 |
| **Matia f.** | **18 Sept. 838** |
| Vice f. | 16 Febr. 847 |
| Jure f. | *(no date entered)* |
| Maria Jurka f. | 24 **lipnja** 852 |

**Three of those dates are this archive's direct line, and all three agree to the
day.**

· **Vicko** is <a>ahnentafel 26</a> — the tree says 17 November 1805.
· **Matia ux.** is <a>ahnentafel 27</a>, Matija Uroda — the tree says 23 April 1810.
· **Matia f.** is <a>ahnentafel 13</a> — the tree says 18 September 1838, and her
  own baptism, read on 10 September, says the same.

**And four of them are new.** This archive had *no siblings at all* for
ahnentafel 13. It now has **Joso (1835), Vice (1847), Jure and Maria Jurka
(1852)** — a household of seven where there was a couple and one daughter.

The month of the youngest is written **in Croatian** — *24 lipnja* — in a book
whose headings are Latin. The same drift the archive found at Novi Vinodolski
and Grižane, here inside a single line.

## The neighbours, for the record

The openings on either side are **Perpić**: *Perpich Pave*, b. 1789, with seven
children entered between 1811 and 1832 (image 46), and *Perpich Bariša*
(image 47). This archive's Prpići are a Krivi Put family and nothing here joins
them — but the surname is at Karlobag too, in the same book, two pages from our
own.

## What it cost, and what it would cost to finish

Finding this household took **a survey of the whole film at low resolution**
plus a read of the P section: the book has no index, and the first attempt to
enter it through a house number failed because there are none. Reading the whole
census properly — every household, both sequences — is **about a hundred
openings**, and it would produce a **dated census of the entire town** twice over,
thirty years apart.

The Urode are the obvious next hour: **ahnentafel 54, Joannis Georgius Uroda**,
born 1777, would have been sixty-seven in 1844 and should have a household of
his own in the U section of the first sequence.

# The Urode, found — and ahnentafel 54 is not among them

Same day, second sitting. The prediction at the end of the last section was that
**ahnentafel 54's household should be in the U section**. The U section is
**images 64 and 65**, and it holds **three Uroda households**. He is in none of
them.

## How they were found

The book's order finally made sense. It is **alphabetical by surname**, and the
two pages of an opening run on from each other — so the sequence is 45L
*Pavicich*, 46L *Perpich Pave*, 47L *Perpich Bariša*, 48L *Pilepich Joso*,
**48R *Pilepich Vicko*** and *Pilepich Maria*, 49L *Pelepich Jakov*, 50–52
*Potocnjak*, 53 *Rukavina*, 54 *Šegota*, 56–58 **Smojver**, 63 *Tomljanović*,
**64–65 Uroda**, 65R *Uzelac*, 66 *Vujačić*.

**The earlier reading that the book "runs alphabetically twice" was half wrong.**
It runs alphabetically **once**, images 8 to about 75 — and what follows is not a
second census but **later additions**, households entered as they formed, which
is why images 76–81 jump P, P, B, B, T, T. Some of those carry dates into the
**1870s**. A ruled book filled in order in 1844 and then appended to for thirty
years.

## The near-miss, and what settles it

**An Uroda widow born in 1789 is exactly what ahnentafel 55 would look like.**
Maria Gerkacs married Joannis Georgius Uroda and was born in **1789**; the widow
at image 64 is an Uroda Maria born in **1789**. Five months apart — 18 March
against 11 August — and the same surname in the same small town.

**It is not her.** The tree gives ahnentafel 55 a death on **6 August 1834**, ten
years before this book was opened, and nothing here contradicts it. The archive's
own August 1789 reading of her baptism stands, with the caution already recorded
there: *natam pridie*, born the day before, and **no day number on the page**, so
11 August is the index's date and not the register's.

Recorded because it is the shape of a mistake this archive could easily have
made: right surname, right year, right town, wrong woman — and the thing that
caught it was a death date the tree had and the census did not.

## And ahnentafel 54 is simply absent

**Nobody in any of the three households was born in 1777.** Joannis Georgius
Uroda would have been sixty-seven when the census was taken. He has **no death
date in this archive at all** — and the conclusion from his absence is that he
died **before 1844**, which puts him inside the **Karlobag death register
1804–1861**, on film and unopened.

That is a better next step than another afternoon in the census: a single
register with a fixed range, holding a death this archive has never had.

## What the Urode do give

**Ive (25 Aug 1811), Vicko (9 March 1816) and Kate Maria (20 April 1820)** are
the right generation and the right town to be the **brothers and sister of
ahnentafel 27**, who was born at Karlobag on 23 April 1810 and had left the house
for the Pilipići long before this book was written. They are **candidates**, and
what would join them is the marriage of ahnentafel 26 and 27 — in the Karlobag
marriage register 1804–1861, which is also on film and also unopened.
