# The Michaels of Otočac, by house number

15 September 2026. Work list row 1, and the answer to **question 4d**: are the
twenty-three Michael Žubrinić baptisms at Otočac one man or many?

**They are at least six men, in six houses, in four villages.**

The 1846–58 Otočac baptism book writes the household into its *Conditio* column,
and the record index gives the image ARK of every indexed entry, so each one
opens at the page instead of being hunted down a film. **Fifteen** indexed
Žubrinić baptisms in that window have a father whose name is a Michael variant —
*Michael · Michaël · Miho · Mio · Miha · Miko*. The work list said seven.
**Fourteen are now read**; FamilySearch closed on the fifteenth.

## What it settles, and what it opens

**The Conditio column separates the Michaels, and nothing else does.** Four
villages — Šumećica, Poljica, Prozor, Obilje — and six distinct wives. No
amount of work on names alone would have parted them; the house number does it
in one reading.

**Šumećica N° 5 is a household this archive did not have.** The Šumećica houses
known before today were N° 2, N° 10 and N° 24. **N° 5 holds a family of four
children across nine years**, and it is the strongest new thread here.

**Three Žubrinić women married Žubrinić men.** Catharina *nata Žubrinich* at
Obilje N° 7, Kata *rodjena Žubrinić* at Prozor Br. 1, and — on the same page as
Stephanus in December 1858 — a Catharina *nata Žubrinić* married to a *Magister*.
**The index carries a given name only for every one of them.** Whatever this
surname was at Otočac, it was marrying itself.

**One of these men was the village headman** — *Senior populi Obilje*, a
standing and not a trade.

**And the index is wrong at least once.** It gives the 1852 mother at Šumećica
N° 5 as «Maria Maković»; the register says **Marković**, the same woman as 1849,
1855 and 1858. A household that looked like two is one.

## Cautions for the fifteenth entry, and for the rest of this book

**The book changes language from page to page, not from year to year.** One
opening of January 1852 is Croatian on the left page and Latin on the right.
*Confiniarius … N°* becomes ***Graničar iz … Br.***, and a search that looks only
for Latin forms will walk past whole stretches of the register.

**The status word drifts while the house number holds.** The Šumećica N° 5 father
is *Graničar* in 1849, *Gregarius* in 1852, *Confiniarius* in 1855 and *Rusticus*
in 1858. Reading the trade as an identity would split one family into four.

**The numbering practice begins inside the book.** July and August 1846 carry no
village and no number at all — *Agricola Confiniarius*, *Sutor*, *Miles
gregarius*, bare. By December 1846 the numbers are there.

**The ARK opens the spread, not the row**, and the entry is as often on the right
page as the left. Twice it was in the last row of a left page, **below where a
whole-page crop stops** — once under the ruled **1855** year heading. And **the
opening does not sit in the same place in every frame**: column fractions
measured on one image land off the paper on the next, so survey each frame at
drop 3 before cropping it.

The ARKs, the confirmed readings and these cautions are in
`sources/familysearch/otocac-michael-1846-1858.json`.

## What the tree already had, and what it did not

The households are **not new people**. Every one of the six wives is already in
the family tree — *Maria Marković, Helena Orešković, Magdalena «Mande»
Draženović, Anna Bobinac, Marija Rogić, Magdalena Glavinić* — and nine of the
fourteen children are there too. A first check said none of the wives was in the
tree; that was wrong, and wrong in an instructive way. They appear only as
**parent references on their children**, with no page of their own, so a search
of published people misses them entirely. **Searching `people.json` is not
searching the tree.**

**What the tree did not have is the house numbers.** Of the nine children in the
tree, exactly one carried one: *Nikola Miko Žubrinić*, born 8 January 1858, whose
birthplace is recorded as **«Prozor 50»** — which this reading independently
confirms. The other eight carry *«Otočac, Croatia»* or nothing at all. Seven are
now in `sources/readings.psv` with the Conditio that names their house.

**And five children are genuinely absent**, now in `sources/found.psv`:

| Child | Baptised | House |

| Maria Magdalena Theresia | 21 July 1846 | *none recorded* |

**Every one of the five has siblings who are in the tree.** These are gaps inside
families the tree already knows, not an unknown branch — which is a much easier
thing to close, and a much better argument that the reading is right.
