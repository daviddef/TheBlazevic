# Štrumbulko occurs once in Croatia, and Shambul not at all

**20 September 2026.** [Row 51](/worklist/) argues that **«Šumbul» is a *nadimak*** — a
house-name written beside the surname, like *Antić **Papa*** at Selce and *Smojver
**vulgò** Vukić* at Karlobag — and that **the surname to search is Marić**. Until now
every support for that came from **inside the GEDCOM**: the string sits in the surname
field, only one of three siblings carries it, and the two sources disagree about its
spelling. Good reasoning about a file, and no evidence from outside it.

**There is evidence from outside it now, and it is a counting argument.**

## A surname recurs. A by-name does not.

Searched across the whole Croatian record index:

| form | exact hits in all of Croatia |
|---|---|
| **Štrumbulko** | **1** — and it is ours |
| **Shambul**, the tree's own spelling | **0** |
| Sumbulko, Strumbul, Sumbulic | **0** |
| Sumbul | 7 — **all in Dalmatia**, all with Italian given names |

**Marić, for contrast, is ordinary at Otočac**: seven in a single page of results for
births 1800–1880, and it is one of the commonest surnames in Lika.

**That is the shape of a by-name and not of a surname.** A family name leaves a trail of
cousins; this one leaves a single entry. And the seven *Sumbul* records are **Dalmatian**
— `Austria, Dalmatia` collections, *Cattarina*, *Giovanna*, *Margarita* — three hundred
kilometres from Otočac and in the wrong language. **The tree's «Shambul Marić» is not a
Lika surname, because in the index it is not a surname anywhere.**

## And the index shows the alternation itself

Two baptisms, the same father's given name, the same parish, **two years apart**:

    3 July 1856      Stephanus Bubrinić   Nicolaus Bubrinić × Anna ŠTRUMBULKO   QKMK-SK94
    15 September 1858  Maria Zubrinić     Nicolaus Zubrinić × Anna MARIĆ        QKMK-3MZV

**This archive already had both entries** — `readings.psv` has carried them since the
Otočac sitting, and the 1856 row is annotated *«the by-name attested»*. **What is new is
that the alternation is visible in the index too**, in text, without opening the film:
the same woman, written **Štrumbulko** in one entry and **Marić** in the next. That is
exactly what a *nadimak* does and exactly what a maiden name does not.

## A third index misreading of a family surname, on the same day as the first two

**«Bubrinić» is Žubrinić.** The 1856 entry is filed under it, and the 1858 entry under
*Zubrinić*. Beside **«Piberich» for Pilipić** and **«Pillopich / Pillepich»** for the same
family, that is **three surnames this index has mangled on this archive's own line in one
day** — and in each case a **given-name query found what the surname query could not**.
Here the query that returned both entries named no surname at all: *father Nicolaus,
mother Anna, Otočac, 1850–1870*.

## What this does not settle

**It does not name ahnentafel 22 or 23.** Ana's father is still a surname with no given
name and her mother is still absent from the tree. **The marriage of Nicolaus Žubrinić
and Anna would name both**, and it was searched for today — by surname pair, by spouse
given names, with and without the parish, across 1845–1865 — and **it is not in the
index.** `Hungary, Croatia-Slavonia, Marriages 1678–1927` does not hold it.

**Nor is Lucca's baptism of 1853 there**, under any of the queries tried.

**So row 51's remaining work is still the book**, exactly as it said. What has changed is
that the hypothesis it rests on is no longer supported only by the file it is trying to
correct. **It stays out of `sources/bynames.psv`**, which holds only phrases read off a
page; an index is somebody else's transcription, and this archive has caught this one
naming the wrong mother, the wrong father and three wrong surnames inside a week.
