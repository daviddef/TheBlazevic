# The naturalisation petitions — four people read out, and a village nobody had

*Work list row 57. 16 September 2026, on the month's subscription.*

---

## Why this record beats a manifest

A passenger manifest gives a **residence** and **one relative**. A petition for
naturalization gives:

* an **exact birth date**
* an **exact birth place** — the town, not the port
* the **spouse's birth date**
* **every child's birth date**

A household in one row, sworn before a court. **And in this collection the birth
place is INDEXED**, which it is not in the passenger lists — so for the first
time this archive can search by **where somebody came from** instead of by what
they were called.

    naturalisations · keywords=otocac    7
    naturalisations · keywords=senj     12
    naturalisations · keywords=karlobag  0
    naturalisations · keywords=novi vinodolski  0
    passenger lists · keywords=prozor    0   ← residence not indexed at all

**But the match is FUZZY and a hit is a candidate, not a fact.** Searching
*klada* returns a page largely of **Greek** names — Coronios, Kallivas, Klapis,
Koromvokis, Papageorgaiopulos. **Every one of the four below was confirmed by
opening the transcript and reading the birthplace**, and that is the only way to
use this.

## KLADA — a village in no parish table, no gazetteer and no person's record

> **Marijan Vukelić**, born **27 January 1894** at **«Klada-senj, Jugoslavia»**
> · wife **Rosie**, b. 31 December 1896 · children **Walter** 3 Aug 1922,
> **Catherine** 30 Aug 1925, **John** 8 Jul 1927 · Brooklyn, Kings, NY
> · **five filings, 1935 to 1948**, the surname also indexed *Vjkelic*

**Klada** is on the coast south of Senj, below Sveta Jelena. It is **not in
`parishes.psv`, not in the gazetteer, and not one person in this tree is placed
there** — the same shape as Sinac this morning, found the same way.

**And Vukelić is not a stranger's surname.** It is the line **Milka Papić married
into**: Zvonimir Vukelić of Senj, whose father Petar was a *gostioničar* and
whose witness Koloman was a *meštar* in the tobacco factory. **This archive has
no Vukelić emigration at all**, and its only Vukelići born 1885–1915 are
Miroslava (1907) and Josip (1913), both with recorded deaths. **Marijan is a new
man from a new village.**

> **Luka Orsanich**, born **15 October 1897** at **«Otocac, Austria»** · wife
> **Anna**, b. 1908 · Declaration of intention, **the Bronx, 1931**

*Orsanich* is **Orešković** — the surname of **ahnentafel 21**.

> **Kate Zubrinić**, born **27 October 1895** at **«Otocac, Yugoslavia»** ·
> petition, Brooklyn, **1941**

The archive knew her husband was from Otočac. **It did not know she was.**

## Two sworn records of one family, and not one child's date agrees

Ivan and Kate filed separately. Both were sworn. Both name the same three sons.

| | **his** petition | **her** petition |
|---|---|---|
| George | 7 January **1923** | 21 May **1922** |
| Joseph | 8 February 1924 | **2** February 1924 |
| John | 13 November **1932** | 8 October **1932** |
| Kate herself | b. 1905 *(1936 filing)* · 27 Oct 1895 *(1939)* | 27 Oct 1895 |

**Three children, two sworn statements, and not one date in common.** A
naturalisation is the most formal record this family left in America and it is
**not reliable to the month**, let alone the day. It is excellent for *who* and
*where*; it is no better than a manifest for *when*.

---

## A correction, made before it was published

The first reading of Ivan's petition took **«Kate Zubrinic Or Rozic»** as *her
maiden name*. **It is not.** The index attaches *«Or Rozic»* to **Ivan** and to
**all three children** as well. It is an **alternate surname carried by the whole
household** — a second name the family used in America — and reading it as a
maiden name would have invented a marriage line that is not there.

## The surname field matches EXACTLY, and that is the third form of the same lesson

Four of this archive's surnames returned **0** in this record set — *kalanj*,
*papic*, *sojat*, *spalj* — and **Miro Šojat is demonstrably in it**, read above.

    lastname=sojat    0
    lastname=sojath   4   ← Miro, his wife Helen, his son George

**The surname box is an exact match, not a fuzzy one** (the *keywords* box is the
opposite: fuzzy enough to return Greeks for «klada»). So **a zero on a surname
means «not under that spelling» and never «not here»**.

That is the same lesson for the third time today, in a third place:

* **Zubrinich / Zubrinic / Zubrinick / Zubrinec** — four spellings of the surname,
  and the fourth was the American clerk's.
* **Perpic / Prpic** — the index spelled a father and his daughter differently
  **in one entry**.
* **Sojat / Sojath** — one letter, and the search is blind without it.

## Kalanj is «Kalan» in America — and the Kalan family is Slovene

`lastname=kalan` returns **20 petitions**, which the 143-record Kalanj harvest of
13 September never saw. It looked like the find of the afternoon.

> **Frank Kalan**, born **10 April 1873**, birth place as transcribed
> **«Krain, Austria»** · wife *Karoline* b.1883 · children *Frank* 1902,
> *Lena* 1905, *Mary* 1907 · New York, 1918 and 1923

**Krain is Carniola** — Slovenia, not Lika. **Kalan is a Slovene surname in its
own right and not a rendering of Kalanj at all.** Recorded so nobody spends a day
on it: **the twenty Kalan petitions are not ours.**

The same for the second Vukelić household: **Louis Peter Vukelić**, b. 18 March
1892, birthplace **«Rogul, Jugo Slavia»**, New Haven 1926 — *not* Klada, *not*
Senj, and no relation established.

## Brinje is confirmed as an emigration source, in the clerks' own spelling

> **Vid Peter Karakas**, born **26 September 1920**, birth place as transcribed
> **«Brinje Plasca, Yugoslavia»** · wife *Helen* b.1930 · children *Kenneth*
> 1952, *Mark* 1953 · Nassau County, NY, **1953**

**Brinje AND Plašćica in one field.** The trial of 13 September found seven
Kalanj manifests giving *«Brinje, Plaščica, Kurjak and Velgovica»* — four places
in Lika — and here is the same pairing thirty years later in a different record
type. Karakaš is not one of this archive's surnames, but **the form the clerks
wrote is**, and [row 50](../../site/src/pages/worklist.astro) wants exactly that.

# All nineteen filings read: fourteen people, and every one of them went to New York

`sources/findmypast/senj-otocac-naturalisations.psv` holds the register. Twelve
filings for **Senj** and seven for **Otočac** resolve to **six and seven
people**, plus Brinje's one — and the pattern is not what this archive expected.

**Thirteen of the fourteen settled in metropolitan New York.** Brooklyn, the
Bronx, Astoria, Jackson Heights, Long Island City, Tarrytown, Nassau County.
Only **Mike Mukulino**, the oldest of them, born 1858, went elsewhere — to
**Boston**.

**That is a different diaspora from the two this archive already had.** The
Žubrinići of **Prozor** went to **Port Pirie**, South Australia, and became
moulders in a lead smelter. The **Kalanji** of Klenovica went to **Leadville,
Colorado** and the **Michigan copper country**. **Senj and Otočac went to New
York** — and none of the three streams touches another.

## The birth country is what the applicant said, and two men from one town did not agree

The same town, written by American clerks across thirty-seven years:

    Senj, Hungary               Mukulino, born 1858, filed 1920
    Senj, Austria               Rukavina, born 1886, filed 1928
    Senj, Hrvatska, Austria     Grzin,    born 1888, filed 1924
    Senj, Yugoslavia            Hammerschmidt, born 1894, filed 1928
    Senj, Jugoslavia            Šojat,    born 1910, filed 1955
    Klada-senj, Jugoslavia      Vukelić,  born 1894, filed 1945

**Rukavina and Hammerschmidt filed in the same year, 1928, about the same town,
and called it two different countries.** It is not the empire at the date of
birth and not the empire at the date of filing — **it is what the man in front of
the clerk said his country was**, and in 1928 two men from Senj did not say the
same thing. One of them even names the province: *Hrvatska*.

**So «Birth country» is not evidence and the transcribed birth PLACE is.** The
same holds at Otočac — *Hungary* in 1927, *Austria* in 1931 and 1941,
*Jugoslavia* in 1937, *Yugoslavia* in 1957 — five countries, one town, one
lifetime.

## What is worth doing next with this

**Every row carries an exact birth date**, which is the one thing that can be
carried back to a parish register and tested. **Otočac births are filmed
1834–1858 only**, so of the Otočac seven **not one is reachable** — they are all
born 1888–1917. **Senj births are filmed 1734–1894**, which puts
**Mukulino (1858), Rukavina (1886), Grzin (1888) and both 1894 men — Vukelić and
Hammerschmidt — inside the film**, to the day.

**Marijan Vukelić is the one to open first**: born **27 January 1894**, the
surname **Milka Papić married into**, and his birthplace **Klada** is a village
this archive has never had.
