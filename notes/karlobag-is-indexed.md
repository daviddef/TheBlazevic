# Row 54 was wrong about its own book: Karlobag 1691–1804 is indexed

*17 September 2026.*

Work list row 54 has stood for days as **«four walls want the same unopened
book»** — later five, when [row 56](../site/src/pages/worklist.astro) added
Antonia Pekass. The whole row is built on the idea that the Karlobag baptisms
1691–1770 are **images that have to be read**, and the session spent hours on
how to get a legible page out of a hidden browser pane.

**The book does not have to be opened. It is indexed, and the index is text.**

The catalogue said so on 16 September and nobody acted on it. The film note for
DGS 5481648 carries **`fs_indexed: "Y"`**, and it was read, quoted and filed
without the consequence being drawn.

---

## What the index actually holds

A search of *Croatia, Church Books, 1516–1994* restricted to **Karlobag**,
**1690–1780**, returns baptisms with **parents named**:

    Ursulu Roxanichu      19 November 1705   father Antonu Roxanichu
    Franciskinu Roxanichu  4 April 1704      father Antonu Roxanichu
    Osiphomu Roxanichu    19 June 1707       father Antonu Roxanichu
    Simonis Bevardich     16 February 1742   Matera Bevardich, Duima Borklacicha
    Andria Bovari         14 December 1749   Ive Bovari, Ivanice
    Toma Bevardich        13 March 1752      Matesse Bevardich, Mande

**1704 to 1752, with both parents, as searchable text.** That is the volume five
walls were waiting on, and the four baptisms row 54 wanted are lookups in a
search box rather than a walk through 327 images.

## And it immediately produced a household

**Ahnentafel 426 is *Simoni Pekass* and 427 is *Mara (Maria) Pekass*** — a
couple this archive has with **no dates at all**, whose only anchor is that
their daughter **Antonia married Josiph Smojver Vukić at Karlobag on 20 November
1752**.

The index has a **Šimun Pekasa at Karlobag with children across twenty years**:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **Margarita Pekasa** | 28 October **1719** | father **Simon Pekasa** |
| **Katarina Pekasa** | 19 June **1734** | **Simuna Pekasa** and **Stoja** |
| **Perina Pekasa** | 29 June **1738** | **Simuna Pekasa** and **Mare** |

**A Šimun Pekas whose wife in 1738 is called *Mare*** — in the right parish, in
the right window, fathering daughters in exactly the years Antonia would have
been born.

**This is a candidate and not a proof, and the difference matters.** What agrees
is a given name, a wife's given name, a parish and a date range. **Antonia's own
baptism has not been found** — a search for *Antonia Pekas* at Karlobag returns
ten records and none of them is hers, so either she is indexed under a spelling
not yet tried or her entry is not indexed at all. Until that entry is read,
Simun and Mare are **the best candidates for ahnentafel 426 and 427 and nothing
more**.

**Two wives, and the archive should note it now rather than discover it later.**
Katarina's mother in 1734 is **Stoja**; Perina's in 1738 is **Mare**. If this is
the right household then Simon married twice, and *which wife Antonia belongs to*
decides whether ahnentafel 427 is Mara at all.

## It also contradicts something written here yesterday

[Row 56](../site/src/pages/worklist.astro) concluded that **Simoni Pekass is at
Smiljan** — from the tree's own `bornPlace` — and that **Smiljan's films begin in
1834**, so *«it does not reach him»*. That conclusion stands on the tree's place
string. **This one stands on a register index**, and it says Karlobag.

Both can be true — a man born at Smiljan can marry into Karlobag, and the
sideways route row 56 proposed (Antonia's Karlobag baptism) assumed exactly that.
But **the reachability verdict changes**: he was filed as out of range because of
Smiljan, and the Karlobag index reaches 1704.

## What this costs and what it replaces

The session had just proved a chunked, hash-verified route for getting a
full-resolution crop out of a hidden browser pane — **about twelve round trips
per page**, with a SHA-256 match end to end. It works, and it is written down.

**It is also now the second-best tool for this job.** A text search costs two
round trips and returns the parents. **Images are for what the index does not
carry** — the house number, the *Conditio* column, the by-name beside the
surname — and those are still worth the twelve.
