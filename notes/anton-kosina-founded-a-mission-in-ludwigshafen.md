# Anton Kosina founded a mission in Ludwigshafen, and led it for eighteen years

**21 September 2026.** The question was put plainly: *if he was a priest, surely there should
be a lot happening here?* **There is.** A priest is a public officeholder, and public office
leaves published records where a private life leaves none. Everything below is from **German
and Croatian Church sources, not from the family**, and it was found in two searches.

## What was already known here

**Antun «Ante» Kosina, born Senj 11 February 1921, died 2003.** Eldest surviving son of Pavao
Kosina and Anka Blažević. **Twenty-two years old when his father was killed in the raid of
8 October 1943** and his two sisters died within five weeks of it. The archive had exactly two
facts about the rest of his life: a gravestone abbreviation, ***vlč.*** — *velečasni*,
Reverend — and a line at the foot of his grandmother's stone, **«OBNOVIO POP ANTE KOSINA ~
1991»**.

## What the published record adds

> **«Na područje Ludwigshafena je 1958. iz Rima došao vlč. Anton Kosina»** — a priest of the
> **then Rijeka-Senj archdiocese** — who settled in the **Kaiserslautern** area. At that time
> **there were only six Croatian priests in the whole of Germany**, so he worked pastorally
> across several *Länder*.
>
> In **1967** the first large wave of Croatian workers reached **Ludwigshafen and Mannheim**,
> which brought him to Ludwigshafen. He was **voditelj — the head — of the Hrvatska katolička
> misija Ludwigshafen from its founding in 1967 until 1985**, and was succeeded on
> **1 August 1985 by fra Ljubo Sesar**, who held it until 2016.

Two independent Croatian outlets carry the same account, both written for the mission's
**fiftieth anniversary in 2017**:

* **CroExpress**, *50 godina HKM Ludwigshafen: Misija danas ima oko 4000 vjernika*
* **Katolički tjednik / nedjelja.ba**, *Ludwigshafen: 50 misijskih godina*

**The mission he founded is now dedicated to Blessed Alojzije Stepinac and has about 4,000
members.**

## Why this fits, and what it independently confirms

The family's own account of him is that he **came from Senj, studied at the Gregoriana in
Rome, and spent most of his adult life working in Germany**. **That account was given
privately and years before any of this was looked up, and the published record agrees with it
on every point it can be tested against** — Senj, Rome, Germany, a working life rather than a
visit. The Church sources say he arrived in Germany **from Rome** in 1958; the family says he
studied there. **Neither knew the other.**

**So the shape of the life is now: Senj 1921 → the raid at twenty-two → Rome → Germany in
1958, one of six → the mission at Ludwigshafen, founded 1967 and led until 1985 → back to
Senj in 1991 to mend his grandmother's grave → 2003.** Eighteen years as the head of a
mission that outlived him by decades.

## What the mission became, and what he seeded

**HKM Karlsruhe's own history names him**, and it is the clearest statement of what those
first nine years on the road amounted to:

> **«Više godina prije osnutka HKM Karlsruhe u tom gradu i okolici pastoralno je djelovao
> vlč. Anton Kosina iz Ludwigshafena.»**

**Karlsruhe was founded on 3 October 1970 on ground he had already been working.** Mannheim,
Mosbach and Speyer were served the same way before they stood on their own.

As the community outgrew one man he brought help — **vlč. Marijan Bevanda (1970–1971)** and
**vlč. Milivoj Galić (1971–1978)**, both priests of the **Mostar-Duvno** diocese. He handed the
mission on **on 1 August 1985 to fra Ljubo Sesar**, who held it for **thirty-one years**, so the
thing he started has had **three leaders in sixty years**.

**It is still there.** About four thousand people. It celebrates at **St Hedwig in
Ludwigshafen**, in the **Franciscan sisters' chapel at Kaiserslautern**, at St Joseph in Speyer
and at Our Lady of Peace in Homburg — **still saying Mass in the town where he began in 1958**.

**And the Ludwigshafen church is dedicated to St Hedwig.** A coincidence, and nothing more: the
parish long predates him. But the mission Ante Kosina founded says its Sunday Mass in the church
of *Sveta Hedviga*, and the first cousin he grew up beside in Senj — the woman this archive is
built around — was **Hedviga**.

## He was a year ahead of the treaty

**West Germany and Yugoslavia signed their labour recruitment agreement at Belgrade in 1968** —
the first between a NATO-aligned state and a non-aligned communist one, and the instrument that
turned a trickle of Croatian workers into the flood the missions existed to meet.

**The mission at Ludwigshafen was founded in 1967.** He had been driving those roads since 1958,
and he moved before the governments signed.

## Claims that are NOT in any source, and are not published

An AI search summary of the same question restates the four real sources accurately and then
adds a biography that appears in none of them: that he reached **Rome in 1945**, that he was
**ordained in 1948**, that his first German years were spent among post-war refugees, and that
the 1985 handover was a transfer from diocesan clergy to the Franciscan Province of Ss Cyril and
Methodius.

A generated PDF, *Anton_Kosina_Biography.pdf*, arrived the same hour and is the same synthesis
exported. It adds no sourced fact and one further unsourced claim: a death on **3 July 2003**, at
eighty-two, which is at least arithmetically consistent with a birth on 11 February 1921. It also
carries a section openly headed **«A Narrative Vignette»** — an invented Sunday morning in October
1972, complete with the time of day, a young man from a village near Split and a cup of tea gone
cold. **It declares itself as invention and it is well written. The danger is not the vignette;
it is the paragraphs around it that read identically and do not declare anything.**

**The tell was internal.** One summary gave his arrival in Germany as **1959** in one paragraph
and **1958** in another, and every real source says 1958. **A plausible story is not a source.**

**These claims are kept, because they are good questions.** An ordination year and a death date
are exactly what a diocesan archive confirms or denies in one line. They are not published as
facts, and the stone gives 2003 on its own authority.

## What is still open

* **His ordination.** The family says the Gregoriana; no year is in hand, and 1958 is his
  arrival in Germany, not necessarily his ordination.
* **How he left Croatia.** Croatian priests of that generation reached Rome by way of the
  refugee camps of Italy and Austria, and the same literature that names him lists priests who
  served those camps. **This has not been checked for him and should not be assumed.**
* **His death in 2003.** The stone gives the year. No obituary has been found online; the
  **Riječka nadbiskupija** and the **Hrvatski dušobrižnički ured** in Frankfurt would both
  hold one, and neither has been asked.
* **Where he is buried.** He is named on his sisters' stone in Senj under **IN MEMORIAM**,
  which on that stone marks the two who are *not* in it.
* **A photograph the family may publish.** This archive has one — the studio portrait in a
  cassock and Roman collar — and no other. **The mission will have more**, and a letter to it
  is drafted at `requests/EMAIL-hkm-ludwigshafen.txt` and has not been sent. Its own website,
  `hkm-lu.de`, is mid-redesign and its history page is offline, which is one more reason to
  write rather than to scrape.

## And a second Kosina went to Germany

**Ivan Christian Kosina**, Oskar's son — the one born in Argentina — **was born Ivan and took
the name Christian when he moved to Germany**, on the family's account. So the two lines that
survived 1943 both ended up in the same country, from opposite directions: **one out of Rome,
one out of Buenos Aires.** Nothing documentary has been found for him and he may be living, so
this archive publishes his name and no dates.
