# Register readings, September 2026

Read closely, from the MyHeritage media library. Each entry is what the document
says, not what the tree says.

## Marijan Blažević — baptism, 1895 (Krmpote/Sv. Jakov register, p.168 no.26)

- **1895, travnja 12 / 16** — born 12 April, baptised 16 April.
- **Marijan**, *zakoniti*.
- Parents: **Jurko Blažević i Tereza rođ. Žubrinić**, ***selj.*** — peasant.
- Residence: **Smokvica kbr. 114**.
- Godparents: **Ive Blažević, sin Tomin**, and **Marija Blažević, ćer Tomina** —
  son and daughter of a Toma, both *m. selj.*
- Baptised by **L. Skalinarić**, *upravitelj*.

**What it gives.** The first occupation ever attached to Juraj — *seljak* — and the
first written form of his name from a record: **Jurko**. And a house number.

## Ivan Blažević — baptism, 1882 (same register, p.37 no.32)

- **1882, 25 / 26 srpnja.** **Ivan**, *zakoniti*.
- Parents: **Toma Blažević i Terezija rođena Pećanić, seljaci**.
- Residence: **Smokvica kbroj 114**.
- Godparents: **Pere Pećanić** and **Marija Butorac**, *supruga Šandrina*.

## Tonka Blažević — baptism, 1884 (same register, no.66)

- **1884, 25 / 27.** **Tonka**, *zakonita*.
- Parents: **Tome Blažević i Tereza rođena Pećanić**.
- Residence: **Smokvica kbroj 114**.

**What these two give.** Toma's household is documented at **Smokvica 114** in
**1882** and **1884**. Jurko's household is documented at **Smokvica 114** in
**1895**. In these registers the house number is the household identifier, and
Toma was himself recorded as born at *Br. 114, Smokvica Krmpotska* in 1838.

## Anka Blažević & Pavao Kosina — marriage, 1919 (Senj marriages p.359 no.44)

- **1919, studenoga 20.**
- Groom: **Pavao Kosina, *trgovac*** — merchant — of Senj, born **15 March 1894**,
  bachelor.
- Bride: **Anka Blažević, *radnica u tvornici duhana*** — tobacco factory worker —
  origin **Otočac**, resident Senj, born **1893**, spinster.
- Groom's parents: **† Martin Kosina** and **Anđelija r. Boras**.
- Bride's parents: **Juraj Blažević i Tereza r. Žubrinić**.
- Witnesses: **Nikola Sudar** and **Julije Pirvan**, both merchants.

**What it gives.** A **second, independent** register naming Juraj Blažević and
Tereza Žubrinić as a couple — a year before the 1920 entry for their son Ljubomir.
It also explains the **Boras** surname in this archive: it arrives through
Anđelija Boras, Pavao Kosina's mother.

Entry 46 on the same page is **Antun Kosina** marrying **Anka Klasunić**, not a
Blažević — the media caption naming "Ruza Blazevic" for that marriage is wrong.

## Josephum Antić — baptism, 1806 (Vinodol/Vrbnik register, narrative Latin)

> *Die 14. Martii A° 1806. baptizavi ego Michael Kombol C.L. infantem lege natum
> patre **Joanne**, matre **Marthe Antich Papa**, nomine **Josephum**,
> susceptoribus conjugibus **Judice Mathia Jellichich** et ejus consorte
> **Margarita**.*

**What it gives.** The **Antić / Papa** alternation attested in **1806**, in the
family's own baptismal entry — the same doubling that reappears as *Papić (Papa)*
eighty years later. And the godfather is the village **judge**, a **Jelličić**:
the family Josephum would marry into.

## Karlobag, and a different social world

Four Karlobag registers, all direct ancestors, all previously misfiled as
photographs because they were uploaded under camera filenames.

- **Josephus Pilipić, 11 March 1768** — *"Josephum filium pridie natum a **D[omi]no**
  Jacobo et Cath[ari]na Pilepich conjugibus hujus loci"*. Godparents
  **D[omi]nus Franciscus de Justiniani** and **D[omi]na Ursula**, widow. The family
  carries the honorific ***Dominus***, and the godfather's name is Venetian
  patrician — Giustiniani.
- **Vicenc Fabian Smojver, 18 January 1754** — and this register is written in
  **Croatian**, not Latin: *"Isti dan kerstih vicencia Fabiana sina Ospa ij Antonie
  Smoiver"*. Son of Josip and Antonija Smojver, exactly as the tree has it.
- **Oliva Maria Smojver, March 1781** — *"ex Vincentio et Oliva Smojver, **vulgo
  Vukich**, conjugibus hujus Civitatis"*. The alias **Smojver alias Vukić**,
  documented. *Hujus Civitatis* — Karlobag was a free city.
- **Joannes Georgius Uroda, April 1777** — godparents **"Illustris D[omi]nus
  Colonellus Joannes Ballinich de Zirfeld"** and the wife of **Centurion Daniel de
  Kalbenaeck**. A colonel and a captain's wife stood for this child.

**What these four are worth.** They show a family in a very different position from
the <a>Blaževići</a>. At Smokvica the record is *seljak*, *nadničar*, house 114. At
Karlobag it is *Dominus*, a Giustiniani, a colonel of the Military Frontier. Both
lines meet in Hedviga.

## Selce marriage, 22 November 1835 — Liber Copulatorum, house 51

> *Ego Vincentius Rondal Pastor Loci Copulavi **Josephum Antich Papa** filium
> Denati Joannis et Margarita cum virgine **Maria Jellicsich** filia Denatorum
> Georgii et Margarita, adstantibus testibus Simone Peter et Vincentio Peter.*

**What it settles.** The groom is entered as **"Antich Papa"** — the two names
compounded in one hand, in the marriage register. Together with the **1806**
baptism (*"matre Marthe Antich Papa"*) this makes **Antić-Papa a single family
name**, not two families conflated by a later researcher. Both his parents are
*denati*, dead — matching the tree's 1829 deaths for Joannis Antić and Margarita.

## Zvonimir Vukelić — baptism, 1883 (Senj, p.77 no.15)

- **Born 13 January 1883**, baptised **25 February**. **Zvonimir Matej**, *zakonit*.
- Parents: **Petar *pl.* Vukelić** and **Lojza**. The **pl.** is *plemeniti* —
  **noble** — and it recurs in the 1907 marriage entry, where his father is
  *"Vukelić pl. Petar, gostioničar"*.
- Godfather **Mate Družić, *poduzetnik*** — a contractor.

**And a correction to this archive's own assumption.** The open question asked how
Zvonimir died in 1915 and proposed the Austro-Hungarian casualty lists, on the
reasoning that he was thirty-two in the second year of the war. But the tree
places his death **at Senj**, and records his occupation as
***gostioničar, bankar*** — innkeeper and **banker**, a rise from the
*gostioničarski pomoćnik* of 1907. A man who died at home in his own town is not
a casualty-list entry. **The Senj death register for 1915 is the right document,
and the Verlustlisten probably are not.**

## Smokvica 114 is a zadruga, not a household — a correction

Reading a **control** document overturned part of this archive's own reasoning.

**Frane Blažević, baptism 2/3 August 1890** (Krmpote register p.126 no.49):
parents **Pave Blažević i Marija rođ. Tomljanović, seljaci**, residence
**Smokvica 114**; godparents **Mate Blažević, težak** and **Luca Blažević,
supruga Nikole**.

But this Pavao Blažević (b. 31 December 1853, Sv. Jakov Krmpote) is the son of
**Luka Blažević (b. 1812)** — *not* of Toma. So house 114 holds at least three
distinct nuclear families between 1882 and 1895:

| Year | Household | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1838 | **Toma** born there | tree |
| 1882, 1884 | **Toma & Terezija Pećanić** | registers read here |
| 1890 | **Pavao (son of Luka) & Marija Tomljanović** | register read here |
| 1895 | **Jurko & Tereza Žubrinić** | register read here |

And the two lines join one generation up: **Luka Blažević** and **Anton
Blažević** — Toma's father — are both sons of **Ilija Blažević** (b. *c.* 1790).
Pavao and Toma are **first cousins**.

**So Smokvica 114 is a *zadruga***: the South Slav extended-family household, in
which married brothers and cousins live under one house number and hold the land
in common. It is the characteristic form of the Military Frontier and of the
Bunjevac settlements.

**What this costs this archive.** It said the house number "is the household
identifier" and used Jurko's presence at 114 as strong evidence that Toma was his
father. That was **overstated**. The number identifies a **kin-group** — the
descendants of Ilija — not a nuclear family, and it cannot single out which man in
that group was Jurko's father.

**What survives.** The godparent evidence is untouched and is the stronger of the
two: the godparents at Marijan's 1895 baptism are **Ive and Marija Blažević, son
and daughter of a Toma** — and Toma's own children Ivan (b. 25 July 1882) and
Marija (b. 27 April 1880) are of exactly those names and ages. Godparents are
*chosen*, and a man's own brother and sister are the classic choice.

## The Papići have no house number — and why that matters

**Anica Papić, baptism 25 May / 3 June 1888** (Senj, p.142 no.63): parents
**"Joso Papić, ***težak*** i Tonka rođ. Perpić"**, godparents **Tone Papić,
težak** and **Tonka Mihovilić**. Under *mjesto obitavanja i **kućni broj*** —
place of residence **and house number** — the entry says only **"Senj"**.

The village registers of Krmpote and Krivi Put record a house number for every
child; the town register of Senj records none. So the **zadruga** that is visible
at Smokvica would be invisible at Senj even if it existed, and no amount of
reading Senj baptisms will show it.

**And a refinement.** Josip Papić now has four occupations across four documents:
*bremenar* (1885), *trhonoša* (1886), ***težak*** (1888), *gostioničar* (1907).
The first three are not three trades; they are three words for a man who works
with his body. The change that matters is the fourth.

## The Boras, and a marriage in a private house

The **Boras** enter this archive sideways: **Angelika Boras (1857–1902)** married
**Martin Kosina**, and their son **Pavao Kosina** married **Anka Blažević** —
Juraj's daughter, Hedviga's aunt, and one of the household destroyed in
[the bombing of 1943](/senj-1943).

**Senj marriages, 9 February 1857, entry 52:**

> *Caelebs **Josephus Boras**, filius Denatorum **Josephi et Annae Boras natae
> Butvalz** — cum **Vidua Maria** … in **domo privata** copulate fuere.*
> Ages **32** and **28**. Witnesses Franciscus Abali and Michael Španj.

Three things sit in that line.

- The marriage was celebrated **in a private house**, by dispensation of the
  bishop — a departure from the parish church that normally means illness,
  urgency or irregularity.
- The groom is ***caelebs***, a bachelor; the bride a ***vidua***, a widow.
- And the couple already had **three children**: Ana and Anton (1854) and Ivan
  (1855). **Angelika** follows the marriage, in 1857.

So this is a **legitimation** — a union of some years regularised in a front room
under episcopal licence. The tree gives the bride's name as **Marija Kabalin** of
Novi Vinodolski; the register calls her a widow, so *Kabalin* would be her maiden
name and the register is using her first husband's. Her age here implies a birth
about **1829** against the tree's **1819**.

The grandmother's name is **Anna Butvalz** — German, like **Ana Untersinger** in
the [Žubrinić](/zubrinic) line. Two German surnames marry into this family in the
Frontier century, which is what an imperial administration leaves behind. And
Angelika's brother **Ivan Boras (1855–1926)** is recorded in the
***carinska služba*** — the **customs service** — a state post in a port town.

## Karlobag, completed — a merchant family

Two more entries close the Karlobag group.

- **Mathia Georgia Uroda, 23 April 1810** — *"Mathiam Georgiam hodie natam a
  **Georgio et Maria Uroda** conjugibus hujus parochiae"*, godmother **Maria,
  wife of Joannes Uroda**. Matches the tree exactly.
- **Matthea Pilipić, 18 September 1838** — *"baptisavit **Mattheam filiam
  legitimam Vincentii Pilipich et Matthea Uroda** conjugum hujus civitatis"*,
  with among the godparents **"Oliva, filia def[uncti] **Josephi Pilipich
  negotioris**"** — Oliva, daughter of the late **Josephus Pilipić, merchant**.

That last word completes the picture. The Karlobag ancestors are:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dominus** | the honorific, at the 1768 baptism |
| **Negotiator** | merchant — Josephus Pilipić, d. 1828 |
| **Godparents** | **Franciscus de Justiniani** (1768) · **Colonel Joannes Ballinich de Zirfeld** and the wife of **Centurion Daniel de Kalbenaeck** (1777) |
| **Of this city** | *hujus Civitatis* — Karlobag was a free royal city, not a village |

Against the Smokvica record — *seljak*, *nadničar*, a house number in a
[zadruga](/bunjevci) — this is a different social order entirely, and both meet
in Hedviga four generations later.

## The four-family reuse test

`tools/name_reuse.py`, run on all four core surnames rather than two:

| Family | Implied infant deaths | Same name, same year | Impossible gaps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Žubrinić | 5 | 3 | 1 (46 years) |
| Blažević | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| Prpić | 2 | 1 | **1 (100 years)** |
| **Papić** | **0** | 1 | 0 |

The **zero** is the finding. It does not mean the Papići lost no children —
**Zora died in 1899, the year she was born, and Stanko in 1903 aged one**, both
*recorded*. They are a **Senj** family and somebody read the Senj death register.
The village families' infant deaths survive only as a name used twice. The table
maps **where the research went**, not where children died.

## The "duplicate" that was not one — Krivi Put were Frontier households

Two scans carry Antonija Prpić's 1859 baptism. They are **not** the same image:
the second is a fuller tabular register with a ***conditio*** column the first
crop did not show.

> **51.** | 12. Maji 1859 | 15. Maji 1859 | **Antonia Perpić** | *legit.* |
> **Joannis, et Luciæ Perpić filia, *Confiniar.*** | Cath. | **Krivi Put N° 136** |
> Lucas Tomljanović, Anna Tomljanović *filia Andreæ*

***Confiniar.*** — **Confiniarii**. And it is not one household: entries 50, 62,
64 and 65 on the same opening give **Perpić**, **Tomljanović** and **Butković**
all as *confiniarii*.

**So Krivi Put was a Military Frontier village**, exactly as
[Otočac](/frontier) was. This archive had the two facts separately — the
[Bunjevac settlement](/bunjevci) planted by the Frontier, and the Žubrinići as
*confiniarii* — without ever showing that **the Prpići were Frontier people too**.
It came out of re-reading a document filed as a duplicate.

## The tobacco factory is where the villages met

**Senj marriages, p.350 entry 16, 18 August 1918.** **Marko Ridjan**, *vojni
obveznik* — a conscript — of **Podvinje, Slavonia**, married **Ana Papić**,
***radnica u tvornici duhana***, born **25 May 1888** — the Anica whose baptism
is read above. Her parents are entered as **Josip Papić** and **Antonija**: one
more attestation of the couple. They married three months before the war ended.

And look at the rest of that page. Entry 11: **Katarina Atalić**, *bivša radnica
u tvornici duhana*, of **Otočac / Šumećica**. Entry 13: **Katarina Vukelić**,
*radnica u tvornici duhana*, of **Krivi Put**.

One page of one register, and three women in the tobacco factory from
**Šumećica**, **Krivi Put** and **Senj** — the three places this family comes
from. With [Milka](/milka), her sister-in-law **Anka**, and
**Antonija Blažević**, that is **six women** of this archive's families on the
same factory floor.

The factory was not merely where Milka worked. **It was the institution that
brought the mountain villages into the town** — and it is why a Blažević of
Smokvica, a Prpić of Krivi Put and a Žubrinić of Otočac end up in one family.
