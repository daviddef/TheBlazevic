# «Joannes Antich Papa» — Selce, 1829, and the two by-names that do not agree

15 September 2026. Work list row 25. **Ahnentafel 48 and 49 are both read**, on
the days the tree gives them and at the ages the tree implies — and each entry
carries a **household by-name**, and the two by-names are not the same.

## The two entries

Selce, LIBER DEFUNCTORUM, image **304**, ARK `3:1:3QS7-L99X-1QQL`.

**Ahnentafel 48**, left page:

> «**Die 13ᵃ Martii 1829** · *Nrus Domus* **51** · **Joannes Antich Papa. obiit
> Annorum 64**, omnibus morien[tium] SS. provisus, et statuto tempore in loco
> consueto per me Vincent. Kombel Parochum sepultus est.»

**Ahnentafel 49**, right page, four months later:

> «**Die 15ᵉ Julii 1829** · *Nrus Domus* **56** · **Margarita uxor Joannis
> Antich <u>Natanchii</u> obiit Annorum 52.** O[mnibus] M[orientium] SS.
> provisa et sepulta…»

**Both dates are the tree's, to the day.** Both ages fit: 64 in March 1829 puts
his birth in 1764–65 and the tree says **1765**; 52 in July 1829 puts hers in
1776–77 and the tree says **1777**. Every field that can agree, agrees.

## And now the part that does not agree

**He is at house 51 and she is at house 56** — both read at 4× to be sure — and
**the by-names differ**. His is ***Papa***, written straight after the surname
with no connector. Hers is ***Natanchii***, attached to her husband's name and
**underlined by the scribe** — and in this archive an underline is the tell that
a household name is being marked, not a flourish.

Three readings, and this note takes none of them:

* **One household, two names.** A village of Antići needs house-names to tell
  its families apart, and a scribe may reach for a different one four months
  later. Selce already gives this archive «*Vincentius Antich, **aliter
  Dudich***» in 1842 — so Antići here demonstrably carried by-names.
* **She moved.** A widow of four months going to a married child's house is
  ordinary; the house would change, and so might the name written beside it.
* **They are two different couples.** Selce had Antići at houses 42, 51, 56, 74,
  76, 87, 121 and 122 within living memory of these pages. A Joannes and a
  Margarita in two of them is not far-fetched.

**What holds it together is the arithmetic.** Two exact dates and two exact ages,
drawn from a tree that did not know the house numbers and did not know the
by-names. What would settle it is the **marriage** — Joannes Antić × Margarita
Lončarić — or the Selce *Status Animarum*, and neither has been opened.

## Why «Papa» matters beyond these two

**Question 5 of this archive asks whether *Papa* is a surname or a by-name.** It
was raised by the Senj marriage of 1885, where ahnentafel 6's father is entered
simply as «**Juraj Papa**» with the surname *Papić* gone.

Here is *Papa* again, **fifty-six years earlier and eighty kilometres away**,
sitting behind a different surname: **Antić**. It is not evidence that the Senj
Papići and the Selce Antići are one family — it is evidence of the opposite
shape, that ***Papa* is a by-name in circulation**, available to more than one
household, and therefore **weak evidence of descent wherever it appears alone**.

That is worth more to this archive than a link would have been.
