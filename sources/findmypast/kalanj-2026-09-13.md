Eighty-one migration records for a surname this archive holds **216 people** of,

**no Kalanj emigration at all**.

## Where they went, and when

Almost entirely to the **United States**, and almost entirely in the two decades
before the First World War:

**1891 · 1901 · 1903 · 1904 ×2 · 1905 ×2 · 1906 ×2 · 1907 ×6 · 1908 ×2 ·
1909 ×7 · 1910 ×5 · 1911 · 1912 ×2 · 1913 ×8 · 1914 ×3**

then a second, smaller wave after the Second World War — **1936, 1947, 1951 ×4,
1955, 1956** — and a handful to **Australia in 1960, 1968 ×3 and 1970**.

They settle in mining and smelting country, which is exactly where
[the Žubrinići went](../../site/src/pages/port-pirie.astro):

- **Lake County, Colorado** (Leadville) — Boya 1881, Rade 1877, Louie 1915, Zora 1916, Millie 1919, George 1922, Sophy 1913

- **Highland Boy, Salt Lake, Utah** — Joseph 1874
- **Duluth, Minnesota** · **Chicago** · **Akron and Summit, Ohio** · **Barron County, Wisconsin** · **Westmoreland, Pennsylvania** · **King County, Washington** · **Reno, Nevada** · **Vermillion, Indiana**
- **British Columbia, Canada** — deaths of Anton 1935, Rose 1930, Anna 1976, Mike Mile 1982

With naturalisations at **Chicago (1942, 1960)**, WWI draft cards for **Mike 1888,
Paul 1894, Steve 1893, Majk 1889**, and WWII enlistments for **George 1921** and

## The caution, which matters more than the find

**These are probably not all one family, and some may not be this family at all.**

Split the given names and they fall into two groups:

| Catholic / Croatian | Orthodox / Serbian |
|---|---|
| Ivan, Joso, Mate, Mile, Miko, Stipe, Luka, Nikola, Ana, Kata, Marica, Maria, Rosa | Bozo, Djuro, Stevan, Stevo, Sava, Todora, Draginja, Milica, Milus, Yovan, Rade, Dane |

The second column is **not** the naming pool of Catholic Klenovica. **Kalanj is
also a Serbian surname in Lika**, and the Krajina sent its own large emigration to
exactly these American mining towns in exactly these years. A shared surname is
not a shared family — this archive has said so on
[every page about names](../../site/src/pages/name.astro), and it applies here
with full force.

**So the honest statement is:** there is a substantial Kalanj emigration to
America between 1891 and 1914, and **some unknown fraction of it belongs to the
Klenovica Kalanji**. Nothing has been attached to anybody.

## What would separate them

Every US passenger manifest after **1906** records the **last place of
residence** and **the name and address of the nearest relative left behind**.
That is the field that decides it. A manifest naming *Klenovica*, *Povile*,
*Ledenice* or *Novi Vinodolski* is ours; one naming a Krajina village is not.
