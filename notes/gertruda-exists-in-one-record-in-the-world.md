# Gertruda exists in one record in the world

**21 September 2026.** Asked to find more on **Martin Kosina** and his mother **Gertruda** —
the two names at the top of the Kosina line, where the tree has a birth year for him, a
given name for her, and nothing else at all.

**The marriage was found. Martin's baptism was not, and the search for it says something.**

## The marriage, and the spelling that had hidden it

> **Martin KOZINA**, b. Dec 1841 — mother **Gertruda Kozina**, ***no father named***
> × **Angjelika Boras**, of **Josip Boras** and **Maria Kabalin**
> *Hungary, Croatia-Slavonia, Marriages 1678–1927* · `1:1:X9DP-5Z31`

**The surname is KOZINA with a z**, and that is why it had never been found. Five spellings
of *Kosina* had been tried against Senj — *Kosina, Kossina, Kozina, Cosina, Kosinac* — and
returned nothing, because **the Senj rows are filed under the Latin place «Senia»** and this
particular entry carries **no place at all**. It surfaced only on a query by *given name and
spouse's surname*, with the surname field doing no work.

**Two things in it are new and one is a caution.**

* **Gertruda is in a register.** She was a given name in a family tree and nothing more.
* **Martin has no father in the entry either.** The tree's gap is not a tree gap — **the
  record itself names a mother and no father.** That is a fact about the document, and it
  points at illegitimacy or a father long dead without settling which.
* **The «Dec» in both birth fields is almost certainly an artefact.** Martin *Dec 1841* and
  Angjelika *Dec 1858*, both December, is the shape of **an age subtracted from a December
  wedding** rather than two December births — and Angjelika's own baptism says
  **1 September 1857**. Her baptism is the better witness; her year in the marriage is out
  by one.

## Angelika's own baptism, which agrees to the day

> **Angelicam Boras**, baptised **1 September 1857**, **Senj** — of *Josephi Boras* and
> *Mariæ Kabalin* · `1:1:QKMJ-SQMC`

**The tree says 1 September 1857.** Exactly.

## Martin's baptism is not in the index. Anywhere.

Searched as **Kosina** and **Kozina**, births 1836–1846, **with the country filter off** —
Croatia, Hungary, Austria, Germany, Bohemia, the United States. **Nothing of his.**

## And Gertruda is in exactly one record on earth

The same one. `q.givenName=Gertruda&q.surname=Kozina`, **no country filter**, returns her
marriage entry and **a burial at Stróża in Poland in 1804**. That is all.

## What the negatives are actually saying

**Two patterns, offered as patterns and not as findings.**

**KOZINA IS A NORTHERN SURNAME.** Swept across Croatia it sits in **Petrovsko, Začretje,
Krapina, Donja Pušća, Jalžabet, Lipovljani, Prelog** — Zagorje, Varaždin, Međimurje,
Sisak-Moslavina. **Not the Velebit coast, not Lika.** The Senj Kosina look like incomers,
which fits a man appearing there in the 1880s to marry a Senj woman.

**GERTRUDA IS A GERMAN NAME.** With the country filter off, the Gertrudas the index returns
around 1840 are **Prussian, German and Dutch** almost without exception — *Jaschinski,
Twardawa, Doering, Abbing, Bartsch, Grochowa, Wismans*. The handful at Senj include a
**Gertrudis Dolenz**. In Croatia it is rare; in the Habsburg north it is ordinary.

**And a fatherless Kozina entry is not unusual in that region.** *Janica Kozina, mother Jane
Kozina* (Začretje 1853); *Franciscus Kozina, mother Catharinæ Kozina* (Krapina 1828); *Kata
Kozina, mother Tereza Kozina* (1879). **Three more mother-only entries in the same surname
and the same country.**

**Together they point north, and this archive is not yet saying more than that.** A pattern
in an index is a place to look, not a finding — and **this family already has a German
surname marrying in twice**, Anna Butvalz among the Boras and Ana Untersinger in the
Žubrinić line. A third would be unremarkable. **It would also be an inference, and Martin's
baptism is a document.**

## Where that document would be

**Not Senj.** Senj baptisms are indexed by three collections back to 1692 and he is not in
them, which — with the surname map — is an argument that he was not born there. **The
Zagorje parishes are the place to look**, and the marriage entry is the thing to read at
full resolution first: a Croatian marriage register usually gives the groom's **birthplace**
and his father's name even when an index drops them.
