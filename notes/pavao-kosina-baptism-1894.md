# The fourth Kosina scan, and the parish wrote his death in the margin of his birth

**21 September 2026.** `4505826-pavao-kosina-birth-1894.jpg` had been in this archive's media
library since **9 September** and had never been opened. It is **Senj births, page 223, entry
37**, and it is the most complete single document the Kosina have.

## Three things this gives that the archive did not have

**1. A baptism date.** The tree had the birth, 15 March 1894, and nothing else. He was baptised
**a month and a day later, on 16 April** — a long gap for Senj, where most of the children on
this page were christened within a fortnight.

**2. THE DEATH, IN THE PARISH'S OWN HAND.** **† 8. X. 1943** is written above his name in the
baptismal register. Until today the only authority for that date was **the gravestone his wife
and sons raised**, and this archive's own rule says *a gravestone is a memorial, not a
register*. **Now the register says it too**, in the book that recorded his birth forty-nine
years earlier. The two agree to the day.

**3. The marriage, a third time.** *«vjenčan dne 20. XI. 1919»* — the same date as the
FamilySearch index and the same date as the 1919 marriage scan. **A reading, an index and an
annotation, from three separate books, all give 20 November 1919.**

## One word is not certain and is recorded as read

The bride in the margin is written **«Antom»** or **«Ankom»** — a single letter, in a hand that
appears twice on the page and nowhere else, so there is nothing to compare its «k» against.
**The person is not in doubt**: the 1919 marriage index names *Anka Blažević*, born 1893,
daughter of *Juraj Blažević* and *Tereza Žubrinić*, married on that day. **The letter is.**

## And the godfather was another merchant

**Mihael Bosco, *trgovac*.** Martin Kosina was a *trgovac* too, and a Slovene incomer; **Bosco
is an Italian surname.** The godparent of a Senj merchant's son in 1894 was another merchant
with an Italian name — which is the town's commercial class in one line, and worth following if
the Kosina are ever traced sideways rather than upwards.
