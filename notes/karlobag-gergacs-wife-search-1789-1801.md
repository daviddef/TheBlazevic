# Looking for a second wife of Michael Gergacs, and for the death of a first one: Karlobag 1789–1801, nothing found

17 October 2026. The question: five baptisms of 1787–1801 name Michael Gergacs's wife as Lucia ([karlobag-gergacs-children-1787-1801.md](karlobag-gergacs-children-1787-1801.md)), and her death in 1809 gives *annorum 34*, which a bride of 1785 cannot be. Either one Lucia with a wrong age, or a first Lucia who died and a second Lucia (born about 1775) who followed her. A second wife aged 34 in 1809 could have married Michael at any time from about 1793, so the search runs to the last baptism, February 1801.

Both books are in the same Karlobag film (DGS 5481649, waypoint 9RK1-HZK; the atlas lists Marriages 1736–1804 and Deaths 1774–1804 under it, so the years are contained).

## What was read

* **Marriages 1736–1804**: images **330–346** (both pages of each), January 1789 to March 1801. Each entry's groom was read; the question was whether Michael Gergacs/Gerkacs/Jerkacs appears as a groom.
* **Deaths 1774–1804**: images **384–405** (both pages), January 1788 to January 1801. The question was whether a «Lucia, uxor Michaelis Gergacs» (or any wife of a Michael Gergacs) died.

## Result

* **No marriage of a Michael Gergacs in 1789–1800.** He appears nowhere as a groom.
* **No death of a wife of Michael Gergacs from 1788 to January 1801.** A «Lucia, vidua defti Vincentii Gerxetich» (Dec 1799) is a different surname; a «Lucia, Mense 6, filia Thomae Paulin» and similar are children.

## Limits, so that this is not read as more than it is

* Read at page scale, about 0.6x of the half-size image, names at the head of each entry; faint or damaged entries (a bled-through entry of November 1799 on image 345) could not be read in full. This is **not a full transcription**, so it is not recorded as a walked volume.
* **No marriages are entered between 27 January and 8 October 1793** (folios 119 and 120 are consecutive); the book may have none for those months, or a leaf may be lost.
* **A Karlobag marriage book holds marriages made at Karlobag.** A second wife from another parish would have married in her own parish, and a first wife could have died away from Karlobag. The absence of an entry here does not rule either out.
* An absent entry is not evidence of an absent event: see the standing rule that only a positive entry is evidence.

## What it does to the question

The baptisms show no change of mother; no first-wife death and no second marriage turn up at Karlobag across the whole window; the 1809 age is plainly «34». The simplest reading remains **one Lucia and a wrong figure in 1809** (a woman born in the 1760s would have been 40 to 48), but the reading is an inference from silence and the two-Lucias reading is not excluded. A baptism of 1815 to Michael Gergach and *Martha* suggests that Michael married again after 1809.
